   b=6 c=(51, 1):    |C|=51, H-C is 7 vertices covered by 3 cliques,

PART 3   why they still survive, and by how little
   Family A (s = 0, sizes (47,1)).  Here R = T u B is only 9 vertices,
   so the second-level split has almost nothing to work with: the
   feasibility cap e(H[R]) <= C(9,2) = 36 forces P up to 594 and A
   carries the whole bound, L(49, 582).  This is the self-similar
   descent case -- C shrinks by 4 while theta drops by 2, so the
   transfer reapplies verbatim on 47 = 2*24 - 1 vertices -- and it is
   still the furthest from Z.
   Families B (s = 22) and C (s = 23).  Now R is 31 or 32 vertices and
   the split is strong: A is a clique K_26 or K_27 of G worth 4724,
   and R carries at most e(H) + P - |A| r + Y_A H-edges, so G[R] is
   nearly complete.  The bound is minimised at Y_A = 47 or 48, where
   the excess left on R is only 4 or 5, so 27 or 28 of its vertices
   are low and Gallai forces a clique block of order 23 or 24.
   The profile is a narrow dip: at Y_A = 25 the bound is 8564 and at
   Y_A = 49 it is 8721, both above Z, but it dips to 7858 in between.
   Closing the dip needs about 450 more at the minimiser, from the
   126 edges e_G(A,R) that the split discards or from a sharper
   crossing bound for a 32-vertex graph at 78 percent density.

CONCLUSION
   The second-level split raises the three surviving second-level
   barriers of the (51,1) class from 4724 to 3783, 7354 and 7858
   against Z(29) = 8281.  Two of the three are now within 1000, but
   none is closed, and the (50,1,1) and (49,1,1) classes do not even
   inherit the transfer.  Order 58 at r = 29 remains open.
