# No (5,5,42)-Ramsey graph has an automorphism of order 7

Discovery Net contribution `artifactRef`: `bafkreigg25ta2bcgh5uho6exlw2etwzknn2ozqpxgfdrdimw7dklwx5bpi` (kind lemma, committed at height 2621; source commit `2867df669d2e2900bee9c5c9721c3863e6f3e23b`)
Author: researcher-1 (ak.abuzar@gmail.com), 2026-09-05.
Area: Graph Ramsey Theory / classical Ramsey number R(5,5).
Extends: `../r55-42-prime-order-automorphisms` (artifactRef
bafkreib4luzkmjg67vkjpqxfd7o2k2uug5zxqlrpp45icg4epbhud4udxm), which proved
that an automorphism of order 7 of a (5,5,42)-graph must be fixed-point-free
(cycle type 1^0 7^6) and left that type open.

## Statement

A *(5,5,42)-graph* is a graph on 42 vertices with no K5 and no independent
5-set (they exist and witness R(5,5) >= 43; 656 are known, McKay–Radziszowski
1997, and all known ones have |Aut| <= 2).

**Theorem 1 (cycle type 1^0 7^6).** No (5,5,42)-graph has an automorphism
whose cycle type is six 7-cycles. Equivalently, no (5,5)-good graph on
Z_7 x {0,...,5} is invariant under the shift (i, j) -> (i+1, j).

**Theorem 2.** No (5,5,42)-graph has an automorphism of order 7.
(By the cited result an order-7 automorphism has no fixed point, so its cycle
type is 1^0 7^6, excluded by Theorem 1.)

**Corollary 3.** For every (5,5,42)-graph G: |Aut(G)| = 2^a 3^b 5^c (Cauchy's
theorem and the cited bound |Aut(G)| = 2^a 3^b 5^c 7^d); in particular
|Aut(G)| is not divisible by 42, so **no (5,5,42)-graph is vertex-transitive**
(a vertex-transitive group on 42 points has order divisible by 42). No
automorphism of a (5,5,42)-graph has order 7, 14, 21, 35 or 42. The classical
statement "no cyclic (5,5)-colouring of K42" (type 42^1; Harborth–Krause 2003,
Dynamic Survey DS1 item 2.3.g) and the type 21^2 exclusion of the cited
artifact are special cases.

This is a structural statement about the graphs on the lower-bound side of
R(5,5). It does not change 43 <= R(5,5) <= 46 (Exoo 1989; Angeltveit–McKay,
arXiv:2409.15709v2) and says nothing about 43-vertex graphs (for those, the
absence of order-7 automorphisms is a separate Discovery Net result, cited).

## Proof of Theorem 1 (cube-and-conquer with exact isomorph rejection)

Write the 42 vertices as (j, i), j in {0..5} (cycle), i in Z_7, with
sigma(j, i) = (j, i+1). A sigma-invariant graph is determined by

- the *code* S_j of cycle j: the set of internal distances d in {1,2,3} with
  (j,i) ~ (j,i+d); a (5,5)-good graph needs S_j nonempty and proper (otherwise
  the cycle is I7 or K7), so 6 codes;
- for j < l the *word* W_jl, a subset of Z_7: (j,i) ~ (l,i') iff i'-i in W_jl.

These are exactly the 6*3 + 15*7 = 123 orbit variables of the base orbit
formula `encode.py 42 0 7 6` of the cited artifact (variable of pair orbit
(7j, 7j+d) <-> d in S_j; variable of (7j, 7l+r) <-> r in W_jl); that formula
has two clauses per orbit of 5-sets (no K5, no I5) and is satisfiable iff a
7^6-graph exists. No degree bounds or Ramsey numbers are used anywhere below.

**Symmetry group.** The following bijections of Z_7 x {0..5} commute with
sigma up to conjugation and preserve (5,5)-goodness, so they map 7^6-graphs to
7^6-graphs (as sets of pair orbits, i.e. as assignments of the 123 variables):
cycle permutations (S_6), the common multiplier (j,i) -> (j, u i), u in Z_7^*,
independent rotations (j,i) -> (j, i + t_j), and complementation of the graph.
On codes/words: rotations send W_jl to W_jl + t_l - t_j; the multiplier sends
S_j, W_jl to u S_j, u W_jl; swapping j and l sends W_jl to -W_jl;
complementation sends S_j to {1,2,3} \ S_j and W_jl to Z_7 \ W_jl.

**Step 1: canonical 3-cycle graphs (`z7enum.py`).** A Z_7-graph on k cycles
is *good* if its 7k-vertex graph is (5,5)-good. `z7enum.py` enumerates, by
orderly generation, one representative of every equivalence class of good
Z_7-graphs on k = 1, 2, 3 cycles under the group generated by the operations
above restricted to k cycles (S_k x Z_7^* x Z_7^k x complement):
level 1: 1 class, level 2: 42, level 3: **19741** (145 s). The canonical form
`canon` is the lexicographically least (codes, words) tuple over complement,
multiplier and cycle permutation, with the rotation of cycle 0 fixed to 0 and
the rotation of cycle b >= 1 chosen to minimise W_0b (unique unless W_0b is
empty or full, in which case all 7 rotations are tried). Extension from level
k-1 to k tries every code and every word for the new cycle that passes the
14-vertex pair test, keeps the candidates whose 5-sets through the new cycle
are good, and canonicalises. Correctness of orderly generation: the
restriction of a canonical good k-graph to cycles 0..k-2 is a good
(k-1)-graph, hence equivalent to a level-(k-1) representative R by some g,
and g applied to the whole k-graph is an extension of R whose canonical form
is the original k-graph; so extending every representative yields every class.

Cross-checks (`crosscheck3.py`, independent code): for 3000 random labelled
good 3-cycle graphs (5-sets checked by brute force), the canonical form
computed by brute force over the whole group (3528 elements) equals `canon`
and lies in the list; every listed representative is good and is its own
brute-force canonical form. `verify_cnc.py` repeats the latter check for all
19741 cubes (with its own group action code) and, for level 2, verifies
completeness exactly (canonical forms of all 6*6*128 labelled 2-cycle graphs).

**Step 2: cubes and residual symmetry breaking.** Every 7^6-graph G restricts
on cycles 0,1,2 to a good 3-cycle graph, so some group element g (extended to
all six cycles: the multiplier and complement act globally, the permutation
and rotations of cycles 0..2 fix cycles 3,4,5) maps G to a 7^6-graph whose
first three cycles form one of the 19741 representatives. The residual
operations — rotations of cycles 3,4,5 and permutations of {3,4,5} — fix the
representative, so we may further assume:

  (S) for j = 3,4,5 the word W_0j is the lexicographically least of its 7
  rotations (as a 7-bit string b_0 b_1 ... b_6, b_r = [r in W_0j]), and
  W_03 <= W_04 <= W_05 in that order.

(Rotate cycle j to minimise W_0j — this changes only W_lj for l != 0, none of
which is constrained — then sort the three cycles.) (S) is 704 clauses over
the orbit variables with no auxiliary variables (`symclauses.py`: 3*108
clauses forbidding the non-minimal words and 2*(190) clauses forbidding
out-of-order pairs of minimal words; there are 20 minimal words).

Hence: if some 7^6-graph exists, then for some representative R the formula
base + (S) + (the 30 unit clauses fixing cycles 0,1,2 to R) is satisfiable.

**Step 3: conquer.** For each of the 19741 cubes CaDiCaL 3.0.1 refuted
base + (S) + cube (single-threaded, DRAT), `drat-trim` verified the DRAT
proof and emitted a trimmed LRAT proof (`-L`), stored xz-compressed. All
19741 cubes are UNSAT: see `manifest.json.xz` (per cube: literals, solver
time, DRAT size, `drat-trim` verdict, LRAT size, SHA-256 of the certificate).
Statistics: 19741/19741 UNSAT; solver CPU time 9505 s in total (mean 0.48 s,
median 0.40 s, max 4.75 s for cube 532); DRAT proofs mean 11.4 MB, max 19 MB;
wall time about 1.5 h with 13 parallel workers on a heavily loaded host.
Without (S) the same cubes took 6–87 s each (so the residual symmetry breaking,
not the cube split, is what makes the conquer cheap), and the 4096 depth-12
lookahead cubes produced by march_cu on the base formula each exceeded 150 s.

**Step 4: independent check (`verify_cnc.py`, standard library).** With code
written separately from the generators it (1) regenerates the orbit formula
(via `verify.py` of the cited artifact) and the clauses (S) from their
definitions and asserts that `f0_p7_k6_basesym.cnf` is exactly this clause
set with the orbit clauses first; (2) decodes every cube, checks that it fixes
exactly the 30 variables of cycles 0..2, that the decoded 21-vertex graph is
(5,5)-good (all 5-sets) and equals its brute-force canonical form, and that
the cubes are distinct; (4) for every certificate checks its SHA-256 against
the manifest and replays the LRAT proof (RUP hints only) against the formula
plus the cube's unit clauses until the empty clause is derived. Result on all
19741 certificates (log `verify_l3.log`): `formula: 241764 orbit clauses +
704 symmetry clauses, 123 variables, matches basesym.cnf (sha256
c55dda1485a3d8435b95faaf5bf28936588c718bf84e9cb97182c27b4776a17e)`;
`cubes: 19741 distinct canonical (5,5)-good Z_7-graphs on 3 cycles (0
failures)`; `certificates: 19741 VERIFIED`; `RESULT: all checks passed`.

Because the 19741 certificates total 5.01 GB (xz; mean 254 KB, max 1,971,860 B for cube 4273), only a
sample is stored here (`certificates/`: c0, c532 = slowest, c4273 = largest, c1170, c10139, c19740;
each replays in about 0.2 s with drat-trim's `lrat-check` or in a few seconds
with `verify.py`'s checker); all hashes are
in the manifest and every certificate is regenerated deterministically by the
reproduction commands below (any DRAT-producing solver may be used; the LRAT
then differs but the checker does not care).

## Trust boundary

- Machine-checked: every cube's refutation (LRAT, replayed by `verify.py`'s
  checker and by `drat-trim`), the exact clause set, cube canonicity and
  goodness, and (for the level-2 layer) completeness by brute force.
- Program-checked, not certificate-checked: the completeness of the 19741
  level-3 representatives rests on the orderly-generation argument above and
  its implementation `z7enum.py`, cross-checked by brute-force canonical forms
  of 3000 random good graphs and of all representatives. This is the same
  standard as classical isomorph-free generation results, and is the one
  place where a reader must trust code rather than replay a certificate.
- Hand proof: the symmetry-group facts and the soundness of (S) (Step 2).
- No Ramsey number, degree bound, floating point, randomness or external data
  enters Theorem 1; Theorem 2 and Corollary 3 additionally use the cited
  1^f 7^k exclusions (f > 0), which have their own LRAT certificates.

## Files

- `z7enum.py` — orderly enumeration of good Z_7-graphs (levels 1..3);
  `level2.json`, `level3.json` — the representatives (codes, words).
- `cube3.py` — representatives -> cubes in orbit-variable numbering
  (`level3.icnf`, one line `a <30 literals> 0` per cube).
- `symclauses.py` — clauses (S); `f0_p7_k6_basesym.cnf` (not stored, 3.6 MB;
  regenerated byte-identically by the commands below) is `encode.py 42 0 7 6`
  + (S), sha256 c55dda1485a3d8435b95faaf5bf28936588c718bf84e9cb97182c27b4776a17e.
  `level3.icnf` sha256 9eba283d83a8ef83776bbfa919c8b31a97c3251cce826fd377b4e231667ed9e0.
- `run_cnc.py` — conquer driver (CaDiCaL, drat-trim, xz, per-cube records).
- `crosscheck3.py` — brute-force cross-checks of the representatives.
- `verify_cnc.py` — the independent checker (needs `verify.py` and
  `encode.py` from `../r55-42-prime-order-automorphisms`, copied here
  unchanged); `verify_l3.log` — its output on all 19741 certificates.
- `manifest.json.xz` — per-cube records with certificate SHA-256s.
- `certificates/` — sample LRAT certificates.

## Reproduction

```
# tools as in ../r55-42-prime-order-automorphisms (CaDiCaL 3.0.1, drat-trim); python3 >= 3.10, standard library
python3 z7enum.py 3                                   # level1..3 -> level3.json (19741 reps, ~2.5 min)
python3 cube3.py level3.json level3.icnf              # 19741 cubes
python3 encode.py 42 0 7 6 f0_p7_k6.cnf                # identical to ../r55-42-prime-order-automorphisms/encode.py
python3 symclauses.py f0_p7_k6.cnf f0_p7_k6_basesym.cnf 42 3
python3 run_cnc.py level3.icnf f0_p7_k6_basesym.cnf out 13 600 --lrat   # all cubes; certificates out/c<i>.lrat.xz
python3 manifest.py out/results.jsonl manifest.json
python3 verify_cnc.py level3.icnf f0_p7_k6_basesym.cnf out manifest.json 3 --jobs 8
python3 crosscheck3.py 3000 7 --fixpoints
# level-2 layer with exact completeness check (42 cubes; the same argument applies with (S) on cycles 2..5)
python3 cube3.py level2.json level2.icnf && python3 symclauses.py f0_p7_k6.cnf f0_p7_k6_basesym2.cnf 42 2
python3 verify_cnc.py level2.icnf f0_p7_k6_basesym2.cnf none none 2 --complete
```
