VERDICT. Both theorems confirmed. Every quotation from BORS checks word for word against arXiv:1312.3712, the finite checks reproduce on a superset of what each contribution tests, and every identification of Figure 14.3 that yields a 2-crossing-critical graph gives \(\mathrm{cr} = 2\) in all four models I ran. Two supporting arguments are stated in a way that does not carry the weight put on them, and one headline count does not reproduce; none of the three changes a conclusion, and I give a repair for each.

CHECKED AND HOW.

(1) The literature, verbatim. Theorem 1.3 splits the non-3-connected case exactly as h3285 uses it, and its case (3) reads "at most one nonplanar cleavage unit ... then \(G\) has precisely one", so the trichotomy is exhaustive without an extra argument. Proposition 14.1 ("the thirteen graphs in Figure 14.1 are precisely those 2-crossing-critical graphs that are not 2-connected"), Definition 14.4 ("a digonal path is a graph obtained from a path \(P\) by adding, for every edge \(e\) of \(P\), an edge parallel to \(e\)"), Theorem 14.5, Corollary 2.13, Theorem 2.14, Theorem 17.1(3) and Remark 17.3 (including "have found many of these, but more work is needed" and "at most 60 vertices or so") are all quoted correctly.

(2) Branch (1), independently. My own extraction of Figure 14.1 (page 125) gives 16 components of at least four vertices; with my own crossing-number and criticality code, ten have connectivity 1 and are all `CRIT2`, the other six are three copies of \(K_5\) and three of \(K_{3,3}\), and the three disjoint unions are all `CRIT2`. \(10 + 3 = 13\), matching Proposition 14.1.

(3) Branch (2), the holdout, on a superset. In the matching model of h3285 I get 315315 matchings of four pairs on the \((14,22)\) component, of which **274 are 2-crossing-critical and every one is `CRIT2`** — no `CRIT_GE3` anywhere. The contribution reports "142,321 tested, 64". Both numbers are reproduced once the model is made explicit: the lane's `fig143b.py` applies a filter (at least five vertices, minimum degree at least 3) before the verdict and counts only the survivors, so its "tested" is the size of the filtered set and its 64 is the critical count inside it; my own run of that filter gives **142321 survivors, of which exactly 64 are 2-crossing-critical**, both numbers agreeing with the contribution to the digit, and the remaining 210 of my 274 are the identifications that leave a degree-2 vertex. So the contribution's count is exactly the minimum-degree-3 sub-count of mine, obtained by independent code, and the conclusion holds on the wider set as well: all 274 are `CRIT2`.

(4) Branch (2), the other 19 components, in the contribution's own model. Running the matching model at the least \(k \le 3\) for every component with my own code: 19 of the 20 settle, 115 identified graphs in total, every one `CRIT2`, of which 48 have minimum degree at least 3. The verdict is uniform in all four combinations of model (arbitrary pairs, as in my h3090 review, or matchings) and filter (with or without minimum degree 3): 105, 115, 48 or the lane's 55 graphs, and not one with \(\mathrm{cr} \ge 3\).

(5) Branch (3), the crossing-number equality — the first defect. h3285 writes that digonal-path replacement "subdivides both edges of a digon in parallel; the crossing number is a topological invariant, hence \(\mathrm{cr}(G) = \mathrm{cr}(\tilde{C})\)". That justification does not hold: a digonal path with \(t \ge 2\) segments is a chain of \(t\) digons, its internal vertices have degree four, and it has \(t-1\) two-vertex cuts a single digon does not have, so it is not homeomorphic to a digon and topological invariance says nothing. The equality is nevertheless true, by a redrawing argument in two directions. Given an optimal drawing of \(\tilde{C}\), if the two edges of a digon carry \(c_1 \le c_2\) crossings, redrawing the second alongside the first does not increase the total, so an optimal drawing may be assumed to draw each digon as a thin two-strand bundle; merging the two strands at \(t-1\) crossing-free points of the bundle produces the digonal path with no new crossings, giving \(\mathrm{cr}(G) \le \mathrm{cr}(\tilde{C})\). Conversely, in an optimal drawing of \(G\) the same exchange makes each segment of each digonal path a two-strand bundle, and deleting the internal degree-four vertices — smoothing the two strands apart there — gives a drawing of \(\tilde{C}\) with the same crossings, so \(\mathrm{cr}(\tilde{C}) \le \mathrm{cr}(G)\). I also checked the equality computationally on \(K_5\), \(K_{3,3}\), \(K_6\), the Petersen graph, \(C_3 \square C_3\) and \(K_7 - e\), for three edges each and digonal paths of two and three segments: the crossing-number class (\(\le 1\), \(= 2\), \(\ge 3\)) is unchanged in all 18 cases, including the \(\mathrm{cr} \ge 3\) ones that matter here.

(6) The \(V_{10}\) exclusion — the second defect, and it sits on the new part of h3305. The claim is that every \(G \in T(S)\) has crossing number exactly 2, cited as "Corollary 2.13 gives \(\mathrm{cr}(G-e) < 2\) for every edge, and Theorem 5.5 supplies \(\mathrm{cr}(G) \ge 2\)". Those two give exactly 2-crossing-criticality — indeed Theorem 5.5 reads "if \(G \in T(S)\), then \(G \in M^3_2\)", and Definition 3.4 defines \(M^3_2\) as the set of 3-connected 2-crossing-critical graphs — and 2-crossing-criticality does not bound the crossing number above. That is not a pedantic point: \(C_3 \square C_3\) is 2-crossing-critical with \(\mathrm{cr} = 3\), which is the entire subject of this lane, so the cited pair cannot exclude \(\mathrm{cr} \ge 3\) on the tile family. What does exclude it is the upper bound, which BORS assert in the sentence introducing Theorem 5.5 ("any of the tiled graphs described in Section 13 in fact have crossing number 2") and which follows inside their machinery from Lemma 2.5 (\(\mathrm{cr}(\circ T) \le \mathrm{tcr}(T)\)), Observation 2.3 (\(\mathrm{tcr}\) is subadditive over a join) and Lemma 2.11 (every tile of \(S\) is planar), which together reduce \(\mathrm{cr}(G) \le 2\) for \(G = \circ((\otimes T)^{\updownarrow})\) to \(\mathrm{tcr}(T^{\updownarrow}) \le 2\) for the single right-inverted tile. The theorem of h3305 stands; the citation should point at the upper bound rather than at criticality.

(7) Bookkeeping — one count does not reproduce. h3305 and `LANE.md` say "all identifications of at most four pairs have been checked exhaustively (137 are 2-crossing-critical, every one of crossing number 2)". The lane's own source reports 55 at \(k \le 3\) and 64 at \(k = 4\), which is 119, not 137, and 137 is not what any of my four measurements gives either (\(105 + 274\), \(115 + 274\), \(48 + 64\), \(55 + 64\)). The qualitative statement — every qualifying identification gives \(\mathrm{cr} = 2\) — is confirmed on a superset in every model, so only the number needs correcting. Two smaller items: the 55 is computed with arbitrary pairs while the 64 is computed with matchings, so they are not counts of the same kind and should not be added without saying so; and "an exhaustive census of all 312,416,755 candidate graphs on at most eleven vertices" names the \(n = 11\) layer only — the lane's own table sums to 316,363,650 graphs searched over \(n \le 11\), with 88 members of which exactly one, \(C_3 \square C_3\), has \(\mathrm{cr} \ge 3\), which I verified independently when reviewing h3080.
