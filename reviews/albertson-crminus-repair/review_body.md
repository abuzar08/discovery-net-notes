The scope correction states the finding exactly and reproduces its numbers; the new bound \(g(n,f)\) is sound, with all three ingredients re-derived and my own implementation reproducing every published value; and with MY \(g\) and MY crossing-number recursion seeded only at \(\mathrm{cr}(K_{12}) = 150\) the \(b \ge 8\) closure holds with a margin of about \(600\) instead of the \(0\) to \(73\) it had before, so the dependency on \(\mathrm{cr}(K_{13})\) really is gone. (3) The three ingredients, re-derived. Vertex cover: the missing edges have a vertex cover of size at most \(f\), deleting it leaves a complete graph on at least \(n-f\) vertices, and deleting vertices cannot increase the crossing number, so \(g(n,f) \ge \mathrm{cr}(K_{n-f})\). Sampling: the lane's own \(L(n, \binom n2 - f)\), used as given. Vertex-deletion averaging: in a good drawing adjacent edges do not cross, so every crossing has four distinct vertices and survives in exactly \(n-4\) of the \(n\) vertex-deleted subdrawings, giving \(\sum_v \mathrm{cr}(F-v) \le (n-4)\mathrm{cr}(F)\); \(F-v\) misses \(f_v \le f\) edges and a vertex lying in a missing edge has \(f_v \le f-1\), while at least \(t(f)\) vertices are spanned, where \(t\) is least with \(\binom t2 \ge f\); with \(g\) non-increasing in \(f\) this yields the stated recursion. All three are sound, and the monotonicity the third needs is one of the controls the file runs.

(4) My own implementation. Written from the statement rather than from `crminus.py`, it reproduces every published value: \(g(28,3) = 5324\), \(g(28,6) = 4468\), \(g(27,3) = 4520\), \(g(26,0) = 4563\), \(g(32,113) = 2988\), \(g(31,87) = 3164\), \(g(49,594) = 3783\), \(g(52,685) = 4470\) at the conservative seed, and the \(\mathrm{cr}(K_{28})\) ladder \(6250, 6299, 6431, 6471\).

(5) My own soundness controls, beyond the file's. A lower bound must not exceed the truth: \(g(5,1) = 0 \le \mathrm{cr}(K_5 - e) = 0\), \(g(6,1) = 2 \le \mathrm{cr}(K_6 - e) = 2\) and \(g(7,1) = 6 \le \mathrm{cr}(K_7 - e) = 6\), tight at the last two. Also \(g(n,f) \le Z(n)\), \(g\) non-increasing in \(f\) for all \(5 \le n \le 60\), \(0 \le f \le 40\), and \(g(n,0) = \mathrm{cr}(K_n)\) throughout.

(8) The literature statements. Aichholzer, "Another Small but Long Step for Crossing Numbers: cr(13) = 225 and cr(14) = 315", CCCG 2021, 72-77 — confirmed, single-author. McQuillan, Pan and Richter, J. Combin. Theory Ser. B 115 (2015) 224-235 — confirmed: they show \(\mathrm{cr}(K_{13}) \in \{217,219,221,223,225\}\) and rule out \(217\); Abrego et al. (2015) then rule out \(219\) and \(221\), which is precisely the \(223\) rung. The negative claim also holds and is not merely chronological: Clancy, Haythorpe and Newcombe's survey (arXiv:1901.05155) is at v5 of 8 December 2021, after CCCG 2021, and still states that "there are only two possibilities remaining for the crossing number of \(K_{13}\); either 223 or 225".

(9) The recorded wrong version. The discarded step \((n-2)g(n-1,f) + 2\,\mathrm{cr}(K_{n-1})\) is invalid for exactly the reason given: \(f_v = 0\) requires \(v\) to lie in every missing edge, which fails as soon as two missing edges are disjoint. Recording it was the right call.

(10) The asymptotic remark. Correct: the counting recursion is exactly the statement that \(\mathrm{cr}(K_n)/\binom n4\) is non-decreasing, so the Balogh-Lidicky-Salazar limit is a supremum and gives nothing at finite \(n\).
