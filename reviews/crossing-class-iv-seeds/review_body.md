VERDICT. Confirmed on every point I can check. All five quotations from arXiv:1312.3712 are word for word; the seed table reproduces exactly under my own peripheral-4-connectivity test over the census, order by order; and the definitional unwinding is correct — I can add that it is the exact trap I fell into myself when reviewing h3080, which is some evidence that recording it was worth the space.

CHECKED AND HOW.

(1) The literature, verbatim. The abstract does say "(iv) determine all the 3-connected 2-crossing-critical graphs that do not contain a subdivision of \(V_8\)", and Remark 17.2 does say "we provided a *method* for finding all 3-connected, 2-crossing-critical graphs not containing a subdivision of \(V_8\). It would be desirable for this program to be completed", with Remark 17.3 adding that the \(V_8\)-containing, \(V_{10}\)-free remainder is unclassified and "more work is needed to find a complete set". So the contribution's central reading — that the abstract's "determine" is delivered as a method rather than a list, and that neither class (iii) nor class (iv) is enumerated in the paper — is exactly what the text says. Theorem 17.1(3) is quoted correctly, including the three-million-vertex bound and the sixty-vertex bound on the part reachable from small seeds, and Theorem 16.14 reads "Suppose \(G \in M^3_2\) and there is an \(n \ge 3\) so that \(G\) has a subdivision of \(V_{2n}\), but no subdivision of \(V_{2(n+1)}\). Then \(\lvert V(G)\rvert = O(n^3)\)", which is the finiteness the body attributes to it.

(2) The seed set, order by order. Applying my own implementation of peripheral 4-connectivity — written from BORS's definition for my h3080 review — to the census and keeping the members on at most ten vertices, I get seeds by order \(6: 1\), \(7: 2\), \(8: 8\), \(9: 10\), \(10: 15\), **total 36** — the published table exactly. Exactly one of them is 4-connected, namely \(C_3 \square C_3\), so it is a seed vacuously as the body says; I verified its connectivity independently when reviewing h3013.

(3) The definitional trap, and a note in its favour. The unwinding is right: with \(k = 2\) components one must be a single vertex; with \(k = 3\), putting each component alone on a side in turn forces all three to be single vertices, since the opposite side then holds two components and cannot be a single vertex; and \(k \ge 4\) is impossible, since a \(2 + 2\) split leaves neither side a single vertex. I record that this is precisely where my own first implementation went wrong in the h3080 review — I rejected every 3-cut leaving more than two components, which is too strict — and the correction there produced the 41-versus-36 reconciliation. So the contribution's decision to write the trap down explicitly was justified by events.

(4) Scope, and what the contribution is careful not to claim. It says plainly that completing BORS's program still needs the patching step and a criticality test on each result, "which is not done here". That is the right boundary, and the two later contributions in this lane — the exact patch list at h3028 and the feasibility correction at h3038, both of which I reviewed in the last two passes — are what took the next steps: the patch list turned out to have 31 members rather than "at most twenty", and the criticality test turned out to be the binding constraint.

(5) The self-correction. The body withdraws its own h2709 wording, which had called class (iv) "the one class BORS determine completely" and placed \(C_3 \square C_3\) in it. Given the Remark 17.2 text that withdrawal is required, and the consequence the body draws — that Vitray's claim cannot be settled by citing BORS alone — follows from it.
