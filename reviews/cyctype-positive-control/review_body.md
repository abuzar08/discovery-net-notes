VERDICT. Confirmed, row by row, by a control I wrote myself — a third independent implementation alongside `cyctype_control.py` and researcher-1's `cyctype.py`. **All eleven cases reproduce exactly**: the same orbit counts, the same clause counts, and zero violated clauses. The two claims that carry real information are also confirmed: **116 of the 328 catalogued \((5,5,42)\)-graphs carry a fixed-point-free involution** — I found them with my own search, and the other 212 provably have none — and the encoding at the real target parameters has **441 pair orbits** with **425334 distinct clause supports**, which is the published 850668 clauses under the two-clauses-per-support convention. I add an explanation of why that count is exactly half of \(\binom{42}{5}\), and one caveat about the closed form anyone cross-checking these numbers will reach for.

CHECKED AND HOW.

(1) THE CONTROL, INDEPENDENTLY CODED. My own routine takes a graph and a permutation, verifies the permutation is an automorphism, reads the pair-orbit assignment off the graph — failing if any orbit is not constant, which is itself a second proof of the automorphism property, as the document notes — builds the clause supports for every \(s\)-subset and every \(t\)-subset, and counts violations. Nothing is shared with either existing implementation.

(2) THE REALIZED CYCLE TYPES. With my own automorphism enumeration, \(\mathrm{Aut}(H_1)\) has order 24 and realises exactly \(2^{12}\) (13 elements), \(3^{8}\) (2), \(4^{6}\) (2), \(6^{4}\) (2), \(12^{2}\) (4); \(\mathrm{Aut}(H_2)\) has order 48 and realises those five plus the mixed \(1^{4} 2^{10}\) (6 elements). That is exactly the inventory the table uses, including the fact that the mixed type is available only on \(H_2\) — so the claim that composite orders and a mixed type are "actually realised" rather than constructed is right.

(3) ELEVEN ROWS, ALL MATCHING. At \((s,t) = (4,5)\), \(n = 24\), my control gives, for both graphs: \(2^{12}\) → **144 orbits, 26598 clauses**; \(3^{8}\) → **92, 17626**; \(4^{6}\) → **72, 13152**; \(6^{4}\) → **48, 8606**; \(12^{2}\) → **24, 4086**; and \(1^{4}2^{10}\) on \(H_2\) → **146, 26728**. Every figure is the published one and **every case has zero violated clauses**. Both graphs are genuine \((4,5,24,132)\)-graphs, which I verified with my own clique and independence searches when reviewing `MR49-LEMMA31.md`.

(4) THE REAL TARGET PARAMETERS. I wrote my own backtracking search for a fixed-point-free involution and ran it over the whole catalogue of 328 graphs on 42 vertices: **116 carry one**, matching the document's figure, and for the remaining 212 the search exhausts rather than timing out, so they provably carry none. Taking one of the 116, I verified with my own searches that it is a genuine \((5,5,42)\)-graph — no \(K_5\), no independent 5-set, 424 edges, degrees 19 to 22 — and ran the control at \((s,t) = (5,5)\): **441 pair orbits, 425334 distinct clause supports, zero violations**.

(5) WHY THE COUNT IS EXACTLY HALF, AND A CONVENTION WORTH STATING. \(425334\) is precisely \(\binom{42}{5}/2\). That is forced: a fixed-point-free involution moves every vertex, and a 5-set has odd size, so no 5-set is \(\sigma\)-invariant; the 5-sets therefore pair off and each pair shares one support. With two clauses per support — the all-positive and the all-negative one — the CNF has \(850668\) clauses, the published figure. The same convention reconciles researcher-1's table, whose 187068 clauses for \(1^{6}9^{4}\) are \(2 \times 93534\) supports. Worth stating explicitly, because a reader comparing the two tables against \(\binom{42}{5} = 850668\) would otherwise suspect one of them of counting 5-sets.

(6) A CAVEAT FOR ANYONE CROSS-CHECKING WITH THE CLOSED FORM. The natural check on these orbit counts is the \(1^{f}p^{k}\) formula \(\binom{f}{2} + fk + \binom{k}{2}p + k(p-1)/2\), which this team has used repeatedly. Its last term is wrong at \(p = 2\): the single internal pair of a 2-cycle is fixed by \(\sigma\), so the internal contribution is \(k\), not \(k(p-1)/2\). With that correction the formula gives \(132 + 12 = 144\) for \(2^{12}\) and \(6 + 40 + 90 + 10 = 146\) for \(1^{4}2^{10}\), both matching my direct computation, and it gives 92 for \(3^{8}\) unchanged. Nothing in either artifact depends on this — both compute orbits directly — but the formula appears in the \(R(4,6)\) documents where it was derived for odd primes, and the cross-check the document describes would fail spuriously at \(p = 2\) if the uncorrected form were used.

(7) The methodological claim, which I endorse. "A positive control is the one direction those cannot reach, because a formula that has lost its solutions still refutes" is exactly right, and it is the failure mode with no internal symptom: a refutation of an over-constrained formula looks identical to a refutation of the right one. The document is also correct that the direct control is impossible here — a \((5,5,42)\)-graph with an order-9 automorphism is the very object being excluded — and it says so rather than substituting a weaker instance and calling it the target.

(8) Scope. "This controls the machinery that builds such a formula, not the instance" is accurate. What the control does establish, and what I confirm independently, is that at eleven parameter settings where witnesses exist the encoding admits them; combined with researcher-1's negative controls and with my own from-scratch reproduction of both order-9 refutations in the review of `db302e8`, the encoding is now supported from both sides by three implementations.
