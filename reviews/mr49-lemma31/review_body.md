VERDICT. Confirmed in full, and the load-bearing step is now supported by a derivation that shares nothing with the original: I did not use McKay and Radziszowski's \(g_2\) identity at all. A direct double count over an arbitrary 24-regular graph on 49 vertices gives \(\sum_v e(G^-_v) - \sum_v e(G^+_v) = 588\) **independently of the triangle count**, and hence the total 12936 and the forcing \(264 = 2 \times 132\). The filter also reproduces from data I downloaded myself: exactly two graphs of the complete catalogue have 132 edges, both are genuine \((4,5,24)\)-graphs, both are 11-regular, and their automorphism groups have orders 24 and 48 with both vertex-transitive, by my own backtracking. CHECKED AND HOW.

(1) THE IDENTITY, DERIVED FROM SCRATCH. Let \(G\) be 24-regular on 49 vertices, \(G^+_v = G[N(v)]\) and \(G^-_v\) the subgraph induced on the non-neighbours of \(v\). Then \(\sum_v e(G^+_v)\) counts pairs (vertex, edge inside its neighbourhood), which is \(3t\) with \(t\) the number of triangles. For an edge \(xy\), the vertices lying outside \(N(x) \cup N(y) \cup \{x,y\}\) number \(49 - (48 - \lambda(xy)) = 1 + \lambda(xy)\), where \(\lambda\) counts common neighbours, so \(\sum_v e(G^-_v) = m + \sum_{xy \in E} \lambda(xy) = 588 + 3t\). Subtracting, the difference is exactly \(m = 588\) whatever \(t\) is — the paper's constant, obtained without its identity. Then \(e(G^-_v) = \binom{24}{2} - e(\overline{G^-_v}) = 276 - e(\overline{G^-_v})\) gives \(\sum_v \bigl[e(G^+_v) + e(\overline{G^-_v})\bigr] = 49 \cdot 276 - 588 = 12936\), and \(12936/49 = 264\) exactly.

(2) BOTH LEGS OF THE FORCING. \(G^+_v\) has 24 vertices, no \(K_4\) (with \(v\) it would be a \(K_5\)) and no independent 5-set, so it is a \((4,5,24)\)-graph and \(e \le 132\). \(G^-_v\) has no \(K_5\) and no independent 4-set (with \(v\) it would be an independent 5-set), so its complement has no independent 5-set and no \(K_4\) — also a \((4,5,24)\)-graph, also \(e \le 132\). With the average exactly \(264 = 2 \times 132\), both are forced to 132 at every vertex. The degree window is likewise re-derived: \(\lvert N(v)\rvert \le R(4,5) - 1 = 24\) and \(49 - 1 - \lvert N(v)\rvert \le 24\) force \(d(v) = 24\), so \(G\) is 24-regular and both sides have exactly 24 vertices.

(3) THE ZERO-SLACK CLAIM, WHICH IS THE MOST USEFUL SENTENCE IN THE DOCUMENT. If \(E(4,5,24)\) were 133 the bound would give \(2 \times 133 = 266 > 264\) and nothing would be forced. So an upper bound that is loose by a single edge does not weaken this lemma, it destroys it — and the document is right that this is the cleanest justification available for insisting on exact table values rather than sound-but-loose ones. It also correctly identifies that a double-counting argument elsewhere in the team's work giving \(e(4,5,24) \le 133\), while true, would not have sufficed.

(5) THE AUTOMORPHISM GROUPS, MY OWN COMPUTATION. By backtracking over adjacency- and common-neighbour-preserving maps, the two graphs have \(\lvert \mathrm{Aut}\rvert = 24\) and \(48\), each with a single vertex orbit, so both are vertex-transitive. The multiset \(\{24,48\}\) is the paper's, and the document is right not to claim which is \(H_1\): that is a labelling question and nothing in the lemma depends on it.
