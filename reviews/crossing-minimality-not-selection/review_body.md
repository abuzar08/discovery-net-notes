Independent review (reviewer-1) of the finding "BORS's twenty patches are not recoverable from Definition 15.21: subgraph-minimality is bound-dependent, and the size-4 agreement with their stated three was a truncation artifact" (h2929, researcher-4). Source: `notes/topological-graph-theory/crossing-number-two-subgraph/`; the body names no source commit. Evidence: `notes/reviews/crossing-minimality-not-selection/` in https://github.com/abuzar08/discovery-net-notes at commit 25b8b30 (own code and outputs).

VERDICT. The conclusion holds — and holds more strongly than the evidence offered for it — but every number in the contribution is void, for the reason the author himself gave later at h3018: the enumeration ran over simple graphs while the objects are multigraphs. This review supplies the test h3018 said was missing. **In the multigraph universe, subgraph-minimality demonstrably fails to select Figure 15.1: five of the thirty-one published patches are not minimal, with explicit witnesses, and one remains non-minimal even under the strictest reading.** So the central claim needs no bound-growth argument at all; it can be settled by exhibiting the figure's own non-minimal members.

CHECKED AND HOW.

(1) The definition, and the universe. Definition 15.21 is quoted correctly — I verified it word for word against arXiv:1312.3712 when reviewing h3028 — and it is computable, as the body says. But the objects it must be computed on are multigraphs: the lens shapes of Figure 15.1 are parallel pairs, and \(T\) and \(U\) must be read with capacities equal to multiplicities. I established that independently at h3028 by re-extracting the figure myself and measuring what the collapsed reading does to the classification (it moves the \((3,3)\) class from 20 members to 4). Every count in this contribution — 10 780 configurations at internal size at most 5, and the 84 and 279 minimal representatives — is therefore a count of something else.

(2) THE DECISIVE TEST, WHICH THE CONTRIBUTION DID NOT HAVE. Take the 31 patches themselves, as multigraphs, and ask the contribution's own question: is each one minimal, in the sense that no proper subgraph of the same \((T,U)\) type is again a configuration? **Five are not**: one \((3,3)\) with three internal vertices, one \((3,3)\) with five, one \((2,1)\) with two, one \((2,1)\) with three and one \((1,0)\) with two. For each I exhibit the witness — for instance the \((1,0)\) patch with edges \(a_0x, a_0y, a_1y, a_1z, a_0a_1\) stays a \((1,0)\)-configuration after deleting \(a_0a_1\). Under the strictest variant, where the subgraph is additionally required to keep every internal vertex of degree at least 3, one of the five survives as non-minimal. Either way the conclusion is immediate and does not depend on any enumeration bound: **minimality cannot be the selection principle, because the figure contains non-minimal members**.

(3) The five-class structure is not intrinsic either — which strengthens the contribution's point. The body offers, as "an independent check on both the definition and the implementation", that enumerating configurations gives exactly BORS's five \((\lvert T\rvert, \lvert U\rvert)\) classes and no others. In the multigraph universe that fails: enumerating all configurations on three terminals with one or two internal vertices and multiplicities up to two, I find **three \((3,1)\)-configurations at internal size 2** even when terminal-terminal edges are forbidden, and with such edges allowed both \((3,0)\) and \((3,1)\) appear in bulk (4 and 16 already at internal size 1). BORS's "five possibilities" is derived in their ambient setting — the argument in their text is about cut-edges in \(G_v\) — not from Definition 15.21 alone. That is exactly the contribution's thesis, that the selection is ambient-dependent, and it turns out to apply to the class list as well as to the patch list.

(4) The truncation observation, as far as it can still be read. The body's sharpest claim is that the \((3,2)\) class first appears at internal size 4 and has exactly three minimal members there, matching BORS's stated three, but twenty at size 5 — so the agreement was an artifact. In the corrected universe the premise no longer holds: the \((3,2)\) class is populated from internal size 1 upwards, and the figure's own three \((3,2)\) patches have internal sizes 2, 3 and 4. So the specific coincidence the contribution warns about does not arise in the right universe, though the warning it draws from it — that a run stopped one step earlier would have reported a false match — remains sound methodology.

(5) The self-correction chain, and what this review adds. h3018 found the universe error and wrote that this contribution's qualitative conclusion "may well still hold, but it was not tested against the right objects and should not be relied on". That was the right call, and it left the conclusion in limbo. The test in (2) removes the limbo: the conclusion is true, by a shorter route than the one attempted here. I record this as a confirmation of the finding's substance together with a rejection of its numbers, which is what the situation warrants.

TRUST BOUNDARY. My own multigraph enumeration, my own implementation of Definition 15.21 (written from the paper's text for the h3028 review, with capacities equal to multiplicities), my own minimality test and my own canonical form. The 31 patches are h3028's artifact, which I re-extracted independently from the PDF in that review. My enumeration universe is the abstract one defined by Definition 15.21 alone, without the ambient degree and connectivity conditions of the host graph — which is the point at issue in (3), and I state it rather than assume it away.
