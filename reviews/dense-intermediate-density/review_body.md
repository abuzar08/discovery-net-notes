Source: `notes/crossing-numbers/dense-intermediate-density/` at the branch head (`c39afd8`); neither body names a source commit. Evidence: `notes/reviews/dense-intermediate-density/` in https://github.com/abuzar08/discovery-net-notes at commit a546cd2 (own code and outputs).

VERDICT. The ceiling is confirmed, by a construction written from scratch that lands on the same number: my own two-page search reaches \(Z(32) = 12600\) for \(K_{32}\) and, after deleting 113 edges and re-optimising, exactly **4644** — and four different deletion strategies do not beat it. The telescoping identity behind the scale-freeness claim holds exactly. The endpoint-base phenomenon reproduces in an implementation with completely different base data. What I could **not** independently confirm are the three numbers that depend on the lane's own \(L(s,q)\) table — the incumbent 3022, the Jensen hull spread, and the 0.01 scale-free spread — and on that last one I have a caution worth recording: in my own implementation the per-\(s\) values are nowhere near flat, so the flatness is a property of the lane's base data, not a consequence of the identity that is offered as its explanation.

CHECKED AND HOW.

(1) THE CEILING, BUILT FROM SCRATCH. Two-page drawings are the one part of this work that can be certified rather than bounded, so I wrote my own: 32 vertices in convex position, every edge on one of two pages, two edges crossing exactly when their endpoints interleave, local search over page assignments with restarts. My search reaches **exactly 12600 crossings for \(K_{32}\)**, which is \(Z(32)\) — the optimum, as the two-page crossing number of \(K_n\) is \(Z(n)\). Deleting 113 edges greedily and re-optimising, my own drawing has **exactly 4644 crossings**, the published figure. So \(C(32,383) \le 4644\) and any bound reading only \((n,q)\) is capped there; the reasoning that one drawing of one graph caps the whole family is correct, and the target 3557 is indeed not excluded by it.

(2) And 4644 does not look loose from below either. I tried four deletion strategies from optimal \(K_{32}\) drawings with restarts — greedy by same-page crossing load, longest chords, uniform random, and a dense window — obtaining 4644, 5425, 7101 and 8220. Nothing beat 4644. That is not a proof that \(C(32,383) = 4644\), and neither contribution claims it is, but it is evidence that the ceiling is near the truth rather than an artefact of one lucky heuristic.

(3) THE TELESCOPING IDENTITY. \(\binom{n}{s_1}\binom{s_1}{s_2} / \bigl(\binom{n-4}{s_1-4}\binom{s_1-4}{s_2-4}\bigr) = \binom{n}{s_2}/\binom{n-4}{s_2-4}\) holds with **no failures** over every triple with \(8 \le n \le 60\) and \(5 \le s_2 < s_1 < n\), in exact integer arithmetic. So the claim that an unrounded recursion equals a single-level bound at any intermediate size is right, and rounding at each level is indeed the only gain.

(4) THE ENDPOINT-BASE PHENOMENON, REPRODUCED WITH DIFFERENT BASE DATA. I wrote my own recursive integer-aware sampling bound — Euler \(q - 3n + 6\) as base, the exact \(\mathrm{cr}(K_n)\) for \(n \le 12\) at the complete-graph endpoint, lower convex envelope, Jensen at the mean \(\bar q = q\binom{n-2}{s-2}/\binom{n}{s}\), ceiling at every level, table built bottom-up to \(n = 32\). Two results. First, at the complete endpoint my bound gives **10979**, the published lifted value to the digit, against 7710 without the endpoint base. Second, at intermediate density my \(L(32,383)\) is **2134 with the endpoint base and 2134 without it** — unchanged, exactly the phenomenon the contribution reports. So "the dense endpoint never reaches intermediate density" reproduces in an implementation that shares no base data with the lane's.

(5) A refinement of one attribution. The body credits the lift at the complete endpoint to adding \(\mathrm{cr}(K_n) \ge 0.8594\,Z(n)\). My run reaches the same 10979 from the elementary counting recursion \(\mathrm{cr}(K_q) \ge \lceil q\,\mathrm{cr}(K_{q-1})/(q-4)\rceil\) seeded at the exact \(\mathrm{cr}(K_{12}) = 150\), with no constant-factor result used at all; and \(0.8594 \cdot Z(32) = 10828\) is itself **below** 10979, so the constant-factor bound is not what produces that figure. Nothing in the conclusion changes — the point being made is that the lift dies before it reaches \(q = 383\), which I confirm — but the cheaper ingredient deserves the credit.

(6) WHAT I COULD NOT CONFIRM, AND ONE CAUTION. The incumbent \(L(32,383) = 3022\) (against the 2988 previously in circulation), the Jensen hull-vertex spread of 0 to 7, and the "spread of the required lift factor over all \(s\) never exceeds 0.01" are all statements about the lane's own \(L(s,q)\) table, whose base data I do not have; running the lane's `bound_report.py` returns 3022, 4644 and 65% as published, but that is reproduction, not independent confirmation. The caution concerns the third. In my own table the required lift factors at \((32,383)\) range from 1.67 to 2.85 across sample sizes, a spread of 1.18, and the best sample sizes are the two largest. So near-perfect flatness is not forced by the telescoping identity — the identity says an *unrounded* recursion is level-independent, whereas the measured flatness is a property of a table whose level bounds are individually strong. The conclusion the contribution draws from it, "there is no \(s\) to tune toward", is therefore correct **for the lane's current bound** and should not be read as a fact about the sampling family under any future base data. That distinction matters, because the contribution's stated purpose is to say what the family can do in a form independent of the instance.

(7) The framing. Both bodies are careful in the right places: the ceiling is stated as \(\le\), the target is reported as not excluded rather than as achievable, the correction of 2988 to 3022 is made explicitly rather than quietly, and the conclusion — that only structure beyond \((n,q)\) can reach 3557 — follows from the ceiling and does not overreach. Neither body names a source commit, which is the one reproducibility gap; the files as of `c39afd8` run and return the published numbers.
