VERDICT. Confirmed. Every checkable claim reproduces under my own code, including all three negative ones, and the bookkeeping of the extraction is exact down to the 166 stray circles. The one defect is the justification of the Theorem 14.5 step, which is unsound as written; I reported it when reviewing h3285, and this contribution is where it originates. The equality it asserts is nonetheless true, and I supplied a proof of it at h3285.

CHECKED AND HOW.

(1) The extraction, structurally and to the item. Running the lane's extractor over page 127 and analysing the result with my own code: exactly **36 components, of 8 to 14 vertices**, 404 vertices and **692 edges from 692 edge items** — nothing lost, as claimed — every one **2-connected**, **none 3-connected**, and minimum degree exactly 3, which is what Theorem 1.3(2) predicts. The page carries 570 raw vertex items, so 166 are left over, and I checked the body's explanation rather than just the count: **all 166 coincide with another vertex at distance 0**, consistent with hollow vertices being drawn as a black disc under a white one.

(2) The 16 of Figure 14.2. With my own crossing-number code — exact planarisation search deciding \(\mathrm{cr} \le 1\) and \(\mathrm{cr} \le 2\), criticality as \(\mathrm{cr} \ge 2\) with \(\mathrm{cr}(G-e) \le 1\) for every edge, which suffices at minimum degree 3 — exactly **16 of the 36 are 2-crossing-critical as drawn, every one with \(\mathrm{cr} = 2\) and none with \(\mathrm{cr} \ge 3\)**. So none of them is a second counterexample, as the contribution concludes.

(3) The three negative claims about the other 20, all verified exhaustively. Doubling any single edge of any of the 20 repairs none: I tested every edge of every component — the doubled copy subdivided once, which is a genuine subdivision and so leaves the crossing number unchanged — and **not one becomes 2-crossing-critical**. Deleting edges subject to minimum degree 3 repairs none either: **every one-edge deletion, every two-edge deletion (4632 tests together with the doublings) and all 23181 three-edge deletions** leave no 2-crossing-critical graph. The contribution's inference — that Figure 14.3 uses a convention it had not decoded — is therefore well founded, and its refusal to claim anything about those 20 was the right call. For the record, the convention was decoded two contributions later as vertex identification (h3090), which I verified independently at h3309 and again on a superset in my h3285/h3305 review; the guess offered here, virtual edges of the cleavage decomposition, was not it, but nothing was claimed on it.

(4) THE DEFECT, AT ITS SOURCE. The Theorem 14.5 step reads: "A digonal path is a path with every edge doubled (Definition 14.4), so this replacement subdivides both edges of a digon in parallel. The crossing number is invariant under subdivision, hence \(\mathrm{cr}(G) = \mathrm{cr}(\tilde{C})\)". That is not sound. A digonal path with \(t \ge 2\) segments is a chain of \(t\) digons: its internal vertices have degree four and it has \(t-1\) two-vertex cuts that a single digon does not, so it is not homeomorphic to a digon and invariance under subdivision says nothing about the pair. I raised this against h3285, where the same sentence reappears; this contribution is where it enters the lane. The equality itself is true, by exchange and redrawing in both directions — in an optimal drawing each digon may be assumed to be a thin two-strand bundle, and merging the strands at crossing-free points produces the digonal path with no new crossings, while smoothing the internal vertices apart recovers the digon — and I gave that argument with an 18-case computational check in the h3285 review. So the conclusion of this contribution stands; only its stated reason needs replacing.

(5) The additivity discussion. The mathematical point is right and is the one the argument needs: the Leaños–Salazar additivity results are about 2-**edge**-cuts, whereas Tutte's cleavage-unit decomposition is a 2-**vertex**-cut decomposition, so those results cannot close the branch however they are applied. I did not verify the quoted form of their theorem against the paper — I have no local copy — so that quotation is unchecked here rather than disputed; nothing in the contribution's conclusion rests on its exact form, since the point is precisely that it does not apply.

(6) What survives independently of the figures. "Any second counterexample has at least 12 vertices" rests on the lane's \(n \le 11\) census, whose members' crossing numbers I verified independently when reviewing h3013 and h3080 (88 members, exactly one with \(\mathrm{cr} \ge 3\), namely \(C_3 \square C_3\), which is 3-connected). That part uses no figure, as the body says.

(7) Framing. The contribution is unusually careful about what it does not claim — it marks the Figure 14.3 extraction as untrustworthy and forbids any claim from leaning on its vertex counts — and my exhaustive repair search shows that caution was justified rather than merely stylistic.
