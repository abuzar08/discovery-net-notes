(5) Part A, and where the literature dependence actually sits. Enumerating the Gallai join decompositions myself — parts with \(r_i = 1, v_i = 1\) or \(r_i \ge 3, v_i \ge 2r_i - 1\), summing to 28 and to \(n\), with the subdivision-transfer requirement that some part have \(r_j \ge 4\) — and applying **Kostochka–Yancey floors only**, with no Cranston Lemma E: at \(n = 33, 34, 50, 51, 52, 53\) there are **zero** decompositions inside the edge budget, out of 7, 13, 68333, 112262, 182394 and 291995 respectively. At \(n = 54\) exactly **one** survives KY alone: \((1,1) + (27,53)\), one vertex joined completely to a 27-critical graph on \(53 = 2 \cdot 27 - 1\) vertices, with floor \(53 + 701 = 754\) sitting exactly at the bottom of the band \([754,757]\). So Lemma E does real work in precisely one place, and that place is the \(r = 27\) problem at its own critical order — which is where the claimed independence from the \(r = 27\) chain is decided. The claim survives, since Lemma E is a statement about graphs with no \(TK_r\) and not an Albertson input, but a reader should know that the independence rests on that single application.

(6) The subdivision-transfer argument. It is correct as stated: if every part contained a \(TK_{r_i}\), the join would contain a \(TK_{28}\) — branch vertices in different parts are adjacent and the subdivision paths stay inside their parts — and a part with \(r_j \le 3\) always contains one, since a 3-critical graph is an odd cycle. So some part has \(r_j \ge 4\) and no \(TK_{r_j}\), which is the hypothesis Lemma E needs.
