VERDICT. The correction is right and I verified it independently: with my own construction of \(GP(4k,k)\) and my own exact skewness search, \(\mathrm{sk}(GP(8,2)) = 0\), \(\mathrm{sk}(GP(12,3)) = 3\) and **\(\mathrm{sk}(GP(16,4)) = 5\)** — so the earlier claim that \(\mathrm{sk}(GP(4k,k)) = k\), which rested on the single value at \(k = 3\), is indeed false at \(k = 4\), and the published correction states the right value. The \(2k-3\) hypothesis is a two-point fit and is labelled as one, which is the correct weight. My \(GP(20,5)\) computation had not finished when this review was submitted, so the \(\ge 5\) at \(k = 5\) is the one row I have not checked.

CHECKED AND HOW.

(1) THE VALUES, WITH MY OWN CODE. I built \(GP(n,k)\) from the definition — outer cycle \(u_i u_{i+1}\), spokes \(u_i v_i\), inner edges \(v_i v_{i+k}\) — and computed skewness exactly by iterative deepening with the search restricted at each step to the edges of a Kuratowski subgraph, which is complete because every planarising deletion set must meet every obstruction. Results: \(GP(8,2)\) is planar, so skewness 0; \(GP(12,3)\) has 24 vertices, 36 edges and **skewness exactly 3**; \(GP(16,4)\) has 32 vertices, 48 edges and **skewness exactly 5**. The first two match the published table and the third is the corrected entry — established from both sides, since my search proves no four-edge deletion planarises it and exhibits a five-edge one.

(2) THE CORRECTION ITSELF. Generalising \(\mathrm{sk}(GP(4k,k)) = k\) from \(k = 3\) alone was an over-reach, and the document now says so in those terms. What makes the correction useful rather than merely honest is that it changes the reading of the DS21 conjecture: \(3, 5\) at \(k = 3, 4\) is inconsistent with \(k + 2\) and consistent with \(2k - 3\), so the disagreement at \(k = 3\) is between the data and the conjecture's *range*, not necessarily its formula. That inference is stated as a hypothesis and the note that the two formulas coincide only at \(k = 5\), while disagreeing at \(k = 7\) where \(2k-3 = 11\) against \(k+2 = 9\), is exactly the right way to make it falsifiable.

(3) The planarity cross-check is real, not decorative. \(GP(n,2)\) is planar for even \(n\) by the standard classification, and \(GP(8,2)\) coming out planar under my own construction and planarity test confirms that the graphs being built are the intended ones — a control on the construction rather than on the mathematics, and the kind that catches an indexing error in \(v_i v_{i+k}\).

(4) The scoping of \(k = 2\) and \(k = 4\). Both are outside the conjecture's stated range of odd \(k\), and the document says plainly that neither bears on its truth, only on which formula was intended. That is correct and worth preserving: a reader who sees a table row disagreeing with a conjecture usually assumes a counterexample, and here two of the four rows cannot be one.

(5) What I have not checked. \(\mathrm{sk}(GP(20,5)) \ge 5\): my run on that graph, 40 vertices and 60 edges, had not returned when this review was submitted, and my cap was 5, so it can only refine the row to "= 5" or "\(\ge 6\)". Since \(2k-3 = 7\) and \(k+2 = 7\) at \(k = 5\), the two formulas agree there and this row cannot separate them either way — the decisive measurement is \(k = 7\), where the search space is a 56-vertex, 84-edge graph and a depth of 9 to 11, which is a different order of computation from anything in the table.

(6) The two-point discipline. "This is a two-point fit and is stated as a hypothesis, not a result. A two-point trend elsewhere in this campaign failed its first test" — that self-reference is accurate; I found the same pattern when reviewing this agent's sampling work, where a per-seed variation that looked real in samples of 4000 draws turned out to be exactly uniform under exhaustive enumeration. Recording the failed precedent next to the new fit is the right practice.
