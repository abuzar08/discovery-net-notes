VERDICT. Confirmed. With my own peripheral-4-connectivity test read from BORS's definition, my own exhaustive \(V_8\) and \(V_{10}\) subdivision detector, and my own construction of the Theorem 15.6 graphs from Definition 15.2, the partition comes out exactly \(36 + 10 + 15 + 4 = 65\) with the same class members. The one class I did not verify independently is the 15 claimed to reduce to a base of crossing number 1; my checks establish that exactly 15 graphs are left for it, not that the reduction succeeds.

CHECKED AND HOW.

(1) Census totals. Parsing the census myself: 88 members, 87 tagged CRIT2 and one CRIT_GE3 (\(C_3 \square C_3\)), of which 65 are 3-connected — exactly as claimed. In my previous pass I verified with an independent exact planarisation search that every CRIT2 member of the \(n \le 10\) part has crossing number 2 and that \(C_3 \square C_3\) has \(\mathrm{cr} \ge 3\).

(2) Peripheral 4-connectivity. BORS define \(G\) to be peripherally-4-connected if it is 3-connected and, for every 3-cut \(X\), any partition of the components of \(G-X\) into nonnull \(H\) and \(J\) has one of them a single vertex. My first implementation rejected any 3-cut leaving more than two components; that is too strict, since three singleton components are permitted — every split of three singletons has a side that is a single vertex — and the lane's own `seeds.py` reads the definition correctly. With the corrected test, 41 of the 65 are peripherally 4-connected, of which exactly 36 are on at most ten vertices: BORS's seed range, and the 36 the contribution calls bases.

(3) \(V_8\) and \(V_{10}\) subdivisions. My own detector, exhaustive by the cubic-branch-vertex argument the contribution also uses: \(V_k\) is cubic, so a subdivision inside an \(n\)-vertex graph uses at most \(n-k\) subdivision vertices, and enumerating their distribution over the \(3k/2\) edges with a subgraph-monomorphism test is complete. Controls pass, including the sharp negative \(C_3 \square C_3 \not\supseteq V_8\), and \(K_8 \supseteq V_8\) against \(K_7 \not\supseteq V_8\). Of the 65, 32 contain a \(V_8\) or \(V_{10}\) subdivision; of the 29 that are not bases in the sense of (2), exactly 10 do — five peripherally 4-connected but on eleven vertices, five not peripherally 4-connected.

(4) The four graphs of Theorem 15.6. Built by my own code from Definition 15.2 — two disjoint copies of \(K_{2,3}\) whose 3-element sides are joined by a perfect matching \(M\), then contracting every subset of \(M\) — there are exactly four up to isomorphism, with \((n,m) = (7,12), (8,13), (9,14), (10,15)\), and contracting all of \(M\) returns \(K_{3,4}\) as Definition 15.2 says. Exactly four census members are isomorphic to them, with those consecutive orders and sizes.

(5) The partition. Classifying in the contribution's order — bases, then \(V_8\)/\(V_{10}\), then Theorem 15.6, then the remainder — my own run gives \(36 + 10 + 15 + 4 = 65\), with no residue and no double counting: the claimed partition, reproduced independently.

(6) Not verified. That the 15 remaining graphs reduce, by planar 3-reductions in the sense of Definition 15.17, to a peripherally-4-connected base \(L\) with \(\mathrm{cr}(L) = 1\) — eight of them to \(K_{3,3}\) — is not checked here.
