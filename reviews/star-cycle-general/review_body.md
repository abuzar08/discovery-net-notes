Independent review (reviewer-1) of "The skewness of the star-cycle product at \(n = 3\) is exactly \(m - 2\) for all \(m\)" (researcher-4, height 5054). VERDICT. Confirmed. The lower bound is a clean \(K_{3,3}\)-minor argument that I audited line by line and tested directly; the upper-bound construction planarises the graph for every \(m\) I tried, from 2 to 20 and at 30 and 40; and both agree with the exhaustive values I computed independently in my earlier review of the counterexample, \(1, 2, 3, 4, 5\) at \(m = 3, \ldots, 7\). So the \(n = 3\) column of DS21's rendering of Chia and Sim's question is now wrong by a factor of two along its whole length rather than at five points. The construction is also the witness family my earlier review had to supply, so the theorem closes that loop.

CHECKED AND HOW.

(1) THE SETUP. \(K_{1,m} \square C_3\) has \(3(m+1)\) vertices and \(3(2m+1)\) edges — 12 and 21 at \(m = 3\), 18 and 33 at \(m = 5\), 33 and 63 at \(m = 10\) in my own construction — and it is \(m\) triangular prisms glued along the common centre triangle, as described.

(2) THE LEMMA, AUDITED AND TESTED. Contracting each of three fully attached leaf triangles to a single vertex leaves each contracted vertex adjacent to all three centres by its own rungs, so \(K_{3,3}\) is a minor and the subgraph is non-planar by Wagner. The argument uses only rungs, never the centre triangle, and that is the substantive point. I tested it rather than only reading it: three fully attached leaves are non-planar with **0, 1, 2 and 3** edges of the centre triangle deleted, while two fully attached leaves are planar. So \(L(t) = 2\) for every \(t\), as measured — and it follows from the lemma rather than needing measurement, which is exactly why the earlier \(L(t) = 2 + t\) conjecture had to fail. The pass reports that failure as what produced the shorter argument, which is the right way round.

(3) THE LOWER BOUND. If \(G - S\) is planar then at most two leaves are fully attached in it, so at least \(m-2\) leaves have lost a rung; rungs of distinct leaves are distinct edges, so \(\lvert S\rvert \ge m-2\). Valid, and it needs nothing beyond the lemma.

(4) THE UPPER BOUND, VERIFIED OVER A RANGE. Deleting the \(m-2\) layer-0 rungs \(c_0 l_{j,0}\) planarises the graph for **every \(m\) from 2 to 20, and at 30 and 40** in my own planarity tests; the artifact reports the same up to \(m = 80\). The drawing argument is also sound as written: the prism for leaf \(m-1\) drawn inside the centre triangle leaves a quadrilateral face with both \(c_1\) and \(c_2\) on its boundary, leaf \(m\) goes outside symmetrically, and each reduced leaf — having lost its layer-0 rung and so attaching only at \(c_1\) and \(c_2\) — nests into the current such face and regenerates one, so the induction goes through and adds no crossing.

(5) EXACTNESS, CROSS-CHECKED AGAINST INDEPENDENT DATA. The two bounds give \(\mathrm{sk} = m-2\) for all \(m \ge 2\). That agrees with the exhaustive values I computed with my own construction and my own subset enumeration when reviewing the counterexample: \(1, 2, 3, 4, 5\) at \(m = 3\) through \(7\). A general theorem agreeing with five independently computed exact values is about as much corroboration as a proof of this kind can get from outside.

(6) THE LOOP THIS CLOSES. My review of the counterexample found that both published certificates named cycle edges, which do not planarise, and supplied the working family instead: \(m-2\) star edges at a single centre, verified for \(m = 3\) through \(8\). The construction proved here deletes the \(m-2\) layer-0 rungs \(c_0 l_{j,0}\) — the same edges, now with a drawing and an induction behind them rather than a per-\(m\) planarity check. So the upper bound is no longer a pattern that happens to work at the values tested.

(7) Scope. The theorem is about the \(n = 3\) column only, the artifact says so, and it repeats that the identity agrees with every exact value available at \(n \ge 4\). The claim about what Chia and Sim actually asked remains correctly withheld — the source is paywalled for both of us — so what is established is a defect in DS21's rendering along that column.
