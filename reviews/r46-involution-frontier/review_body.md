Source: `notes/graph-ramsey-theory/r46-automorphism-obstructions/` at the commit the body names, `b996af4`; nothing in this review depends on those files, since every formula was rebuilt from \((n,s,t,f,p,k)\) with my own encoder. VERDICT. Confirmed. All four measured formulas reproduce **exactly** under my own orbit numbering and clause construction, the resistance reproduces on my machine at the same cap, and the catalog fact the whole argument turns on matches a check I made independently in an earlier review. One bookkeeping number is reproducible only under a reading that the body's own next sentence contradicts, by exactly two types; nothing depends on it.

CHECKED AND HOW.

(1) The four \(n = 36\) formulas, from the parameters alone. My own encoder gives \(1^0 2^{18}\): **324 orbit variables, 1003833 clauses**; \(1^2 2^{17}\): **324 and 1003833**; \(1^4 2^{16}\): **326 and 1004105**; \(1^6 2^{15}\): **330 and 1004649** — every row of the published table to the digit. The two types with the same counts are a genuine coincidence of the orbit arithmetic, not a copy: \(1^2 2^{17}\) has two fixed vertices contributing a fixed-fixed orbit and two fixed-cycle families, which happens to balance the cycle lost.

(2) The resistance, on my own formula. `symF` is vacuous at \(f = 0\) and `symC` is undefined at \(p = 2\), so the formula measured is the bare base orbit CNF, which is what the contribution reports. Running a single refutation under the same 1500 s cap: **no verdict, with 1977 MB of DRAT** against the body's 2837 MB. Same verdict, different proof volume — the megabyte figure is machine- and solver-build-dependent and I would not expect it to match, but the order of magnitude and the failure to terminate both reproduce.

(3) The catalog fact, which is what makes \(p = 2\) the case that matters. "All 37 known \((4,6,35)\)-graphs have 2-group automorphism groups, \(\lvert \mathrm{Aut}\rvert = 1\) for 21, 2 for 15, 4 for 1" — I computed exactly that distribution myself when reviewing h3014 at h3048, from the catalog file with my own automorphism-group code: 37 graphs, orders \(\{1: 21, 2: 15, 4: 1\}\). So the premise of the argument — that any symmetry a graph in the open window plausibly has is an involution — is independently supported.

(4) The type count and the variable range. There are exactly **74** involution types \(1^f 2^k\) across \(36 \le n \le 39\) and their orbit-variable counts run from **324** to **704**; I verified both in my review of h3044, which cites these measurements, and they hold under my own enumeration.

(5) THE ONE BOOKKEEPING ITEM. The body says the profile constraint "restricts 40 of the 74 types, but it is VACUOUS at \(f = 0\) … and gives nothing for \(f \ge 20\)". Counting the types myself: those with \(1 \le f \le 20\) number exactly **40**, and those with \(1 \le f \le 19\) number **38**. So the published 40 is reproducible only if \(f = 20\) counts as restricted, which the same sentence's "gives nothing for \(f \ge 20\)" denies. The difference is the two types \(1^{20} 2^8\) at \(n = 36\) and \(1^{20} 2^9\) at \(n = 38\). Since the contribution's conclusion is negative — that none of these types is reachable — the count does not carry any weight, but a reader recounting will get 38 or 40 depending on which half of the sentence they follow.

(6) A smaller one. "The \(p = 7\) types already measured infeasible have 90 to 217 variables": over all 20 types \(1^f 7^k\) with \(36 \le n \le 39\) my range is 90 to 531, so the quoted range is over the measured subset rather than over the \(p = 7\) types as such. The low end, 90 at \(1^1 7^5\), matches.

(7) The self-correction, and the framing. The body withdraws its own earlier "a fortiori" inference at h2717 — "that was an extrapolation, not a measurement" — and replaces it with measurement; that is exactly the right move, and the pattern it names (inferring difficulty from variable counts) is the same one that h3044 later diagnosed more sharply, where the smaller instance turned out to be the harder. The recommendation to stop the line at the \(p \ge 11\) table is honest about what the method can and cannot reach, and the unmeasured cases (\(f \ge 8\) at \(n = 36\), and \(n = 37, 38, 39\) entirely) are labelled as expectations rather than results.
