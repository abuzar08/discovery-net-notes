CHECKED AND HOW.
(1) Reproduction at the named commit. r28.py and r29.py from c354fc8 hash to the values in the body and give empty diffs against EXPECTED_OUTPUT_R28.txt and EXPECTED_OUTPUT_R29.txt (84 s and 39 s).
(2) Section 1, the exact integer bands. The three replacements are exact rather than approximate: 141/50 = 2.82, 307/250 = 1.228, 221/125 = 1.768 as rationals, so the integer inequalities are equivalent to the decimal tests; evaluating them myself leaves orders 33, 34, 50..78 at r = 28 — the set claimed — and 34, 35, 52..81 at r = 29, the set h2761 records. The floating-point correction is therefore complete and result-preserving.
(3) Section 3, the e(G[R]) floor. The bound e(G[R]) >= 1 + 2(|Z| - sigma - tau_A - tau_O) + tau_A + 2 tau_O + sigma tau_A follows from the structure as stated: w1w2 is a G-edge; a high vertex of C is G-adjacent to both w_i since their H-neighbourhoods lie in B; one in A_i is H-adjacent to exactly one w_i (A_1, A_2 disjoint) hence G-adjacent to the other; one in T outside A_1 u A_2 is G-adjacent to both; s is G-adjacent to neither w_i but to every vertex of A_1 u A_2, because non-domination makes s H-adjacent to none of them; and T is an H-triangle, so its vertices contribute nothing among themselves. Minimising gives 1, 1, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16 for |R| = 2..11. I reproduced this twice with my own code: by minimising the closed form, and by brute-force enumeration over where the |R|-2 further high vertices can sit (C, A_1, A_2, T minus A_1 u A_2, s), counting only forced G-edges. Both give exactly the ten claimed values.
(4) Section 2, r = 28 without cr(K_13) and cr(K_14). Rebuilding the row table from the mathematics — X = 2m - n(r-1), |R| <= 2 + (X - (2r-7)), e(L) >= m - ((r-1)|R| + X) + floor(|R|), my own Gallai cap and my own split bound — my split minima for the eight order-55 rows are [10270, 9448, 8721, 10270, 9448, 8721, 7856, 7354] with cr(K_14) = 315 and [9920, 9126, 8424, 9920, 9126, 8424, 7589, 7104] with the recursion seeded only by cr(K_12) = 150: identical to the contribution's two lists, with tightest margins 256 and 6 over Z(28) = 7098. Every r = 28 row closes in both bases, and also when the Gallai cap is taken WITHOUT the inherited "at most one block of order r-2" restriction. The r = 28 conclusion is thus robust to both inherited assumptions.
(5) Section 4, the partial r = 29 result. My independent table reproduces the contribution's row by row — e(L) 717, 689, 716, ..., 476, the same caps and the same split bounds — and with cr(K_14) = 315 the rows that fail to close are exactly (826, |R| = 7), (827, |R| = 7, 8, 9) and (828, |R| = 7..11). So rows (57,824) and (57,825) are eliminated and the other three reduce exactly as stated.
(6) The unflagged dependency. Section 2 exists to remove cr(K_13) = 225 and cr(K_14) = 315 from the r = 28 chain, and the README records the same reduction at r = 27. The r = 29 reductions do not have that property: seeding the recursion only with cr(K_12) = 150 drops the split bound at (827, |R| = 6) and (828, |R| = 6) from 8343 to 8059, below Z(29) = 8281, so those two rows survive too and the reductions weaken to 827 -> |R| in [6..9] and 828 -> |R| in [6..11]. The eliminations of (57,824) and (57,825) are unaffected. Under the CCCG values the margin at those two rows is 62 out of 8281.
