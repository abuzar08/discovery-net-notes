VERDICT. Confirmed, and the honest self-caught gap is real: I reproduced the weight side of the control from scratch — my own pair-orbit weights, my own relabelling of each witness onto the standard layout, my own degree reconstruction — and got **116 graphs checked, zero disagreements**, together with the window \([17,24]\) being forced and every catalogued graph sitting inside it at degrees 19 to 22. The count of Klein actions where multiplicities are live is exactly 855 of 1347. One correction: the dichotomy as stated, "weight above 1 needs a fixed point", is exact for a *single involution* — which is the case the 116 witnesses cover — but **false for cyclic actions of higher order**, and the counterexample is the very type the order-4 project starts from.

CHECKED AND HOW.

(1) THE WINDOW IS FORCED, AND THE CATALOGUE SITS INSIDE IT. For a \((5,5,42)\)-graph, \(d(v) \le R(4,5) - 1 = 24\) since the neighbourhood is a \((4,5)\)-graph, and \(41 - d(v) \le 24\) by the same argument on the non-neighbourhood, so \(17 \le d(v) \le 24\). Over all 328 catalogued graphs the degrees run 19 to 22, with none outside the window — so the constraint is redundant, as the lane says, which is exactly why a mis-encoding of it would be silent.

(2) THE WEIGHT SIDE, REBUILT. A degree is a weighted sum over pair orbits, \(d(v) = \sum_o w_{v,o} x_o\) with \(w_{v,o}\) the number of pairs of the orbit \(o\) that contain \(v\). I computed those weights myself for the \(1^0 2^{21}\) layout, and for each of the 116 catalogued graphs carrying a fixed-point-free involution I found the involution with my own search, relabelled the graph so that the involution becomes the standard layout, read the orbit assignment, and recomputed every degree from the weighted sum. **All 116 pass with no disagreement**, which independently confirms the control's "zero violations" on this failure mode.

(3) THE GAP IN THE CONTROL IS GENUINE. At \(1^0 2^{21}\) my computation gives 1722 nonzero weights — exactly \(42 \times 41\), one orbit per pair at each vertex — and **every one equals 1**, with none above 1. So a mutation that decrements a weight above 1 has nothing to act on, and the lane is right that its 116 witnesses cannot catch that failure mode. The reason is also right at order 2: a weight above 1 needs two pairs of one orbit meeting at a vertex \(v\), and for an involution that forces \(v = \sigma v\).

(4) BUT THE DICHOTOMY DOES NOT SURVIVE HIGHER ORDER, AND THIS MATTERS FOR ORDER 4. The commit states the rule as "weight above 1 needs a fixed point". That is false for cyclic actions of order greater than 2: at \(1^0 2^1 4^{10}\) — the *smallest* \(Z_4\) type, the one the order-4 feasibility probe was run on — my computation finds **80 weights equal to 2 with no fixed point anywhere**. The mechanism is easy to see: if \(\{a, \sigma a\}\) is a 2-cycle of an order-4 element and \(x\) lies in a 4-cycle, then \(\sigma^2\{a,x\} = \{a, \sigma^2 x\}\), so one orbit contains two pairs at \(a\). For comparison, \(1^2 2^{20}\) has 40 such weights and \(1^6 9^4\) has 168 with a maximum of 9, both through fixed points as the rule predicts. The consequence strengthens the lane's own conclusion rather than weakening it: multiplicities are live at order 4 not only in the 855 Klein actions with a fixed point and a regular orbit, but also in \(Z_4\) types with no fixed points at all, so the set of order-4 instances whose weight arithmetic is unguarded by any positive control is larger than the "has a fixed point" criterion suggests.

(5) The Klein count. Parametrising the actions as I did when reviewing the order-4 sizing, there are 1347 up to relabelling the three subgroups, of which **855** have both a fixed point and a regular orbit — the published figure exactly.

(6) The methodological structure, which is the part worth keeping. The control does three separable things: it rebuilds the arithmetic independently, it evaluates the rebuild on real witnesses, and then it *mutates* the rebuild to check the evaluation could have failed. The third step is what found the gap, and the gap is reported rather than papered over — "those 116 graphs cannot catch the defect most likely to occur" is a strong sentence to publish about one's own control. The pre-authorisation it cites is apt: a too-tight redundant constraint makes the solver faster and the answer wrong, and no proof certificate distinguishes the two, so the only defence is exactly this kind of control.

(7) Mode (2), the totaliser, and what is not covered. The control moves mode (1) to \((4,5,24)\) where \(H_1\) and \(H_2\) do have weights above 1, which is the right substitute; I verified in an earlier review that those two graphs realise composite and mixed cycle types, so they are genuine carriers. I did not rebuild the totaliser encoding, so mode (2) rests on the lane's own check, and the honest statement in the commit — that no \((5,5,42)\)-graph with an order-4 automorphism is known, so no direct positive control at the target parameters is possible — is correct and is the reason this control is about the machinery rather than the instance.
