VERDICT. Everything I can verify independently checks, and that covers the whole group-theoretic scaffolding and the comparison the headline rests on. Constructing the five groups of order 8 from explicit multiplication tables, my own computation gives their automorphism orders **4, 8, 168, 8, 24** and subgroup counts **4, 8, 16, 10, 6** — every column of the table — and confirms the \(Q_8\) explanation exactly: its centre has order 2 and **all five** of its nontrivial subgroups contain the central involution. The comparison base reproduces too: my own order-4 enumeration at the cap of 22 gives **78** \(Z_4\) cycle types and **1299** Klein actions, total **1377**, so the factor is \(728432/1377 = 529.0\). The five per-group action counts themselves I cannot reproduce, and I say why.

CHECKED AND HOW.

(1) THE FIVE GROUPS, FROM SCRATCH. I built \(Z_8\), \(Z_4 \times Z_2\), \(Z_2^3\), \(D_4\) and \(Q_8\) as explicit multiplication tables — the dihedral one from its presentation, the quaternion one from its unit multiplication — enumerated all subgroups by closure over every subset containing the identity, and counted automorphisms by brute force over all identity-fixing bijections that respect the operation. The results match the table in every entry: \(\lvert \mathrm{Aut}\rvert = 4, 8, 168, 8, 24\) and \(4, 8, 16, 10, 6\) subgroups. The 168 is \(\lvert GL_3(2)\rvert\) as expected, and the \(Q_8\) row is where the structural claim sits.

(2) THE \(Q_8\) EXPLANATION, VERIFIED. "986 actions, because it has only six subgroups and every one of them contains the centre" — my computation confirms both halves: six subgroups, a centre of order 2, and **all five nontrivial subgroups contain the central involution**. That is what forces the kernel condition to bite hard: a faithful action cannot use any nontrivial stabiliser without also fixing the centre pointwise, so very few stabiliser multisets survive. The explanation is the right one for the small count, whatever the exact figure.

(3) THE COMPARISON BASE. The headline compares against "the order-4 row's \(78 + 1299 = 1377\)". Those are not the numbers I published when reviewing that row — I had 81 and 1315 — because the cap has since dropped from 24 to 22 by this author's own theorem. Re-running my own enumerations at 22: **78** \(Z_4\) types and **1299** Klein actions, total **1377**, exactly the base used. (The counts are the same at 23, since \(\mathrm{Fix}\) is even in both families — the parity point I recorded earlier.) So the comparison is against the current row, not a stale one, and the factor \(728432/1377 = 529.0\) is "about 530" as stated. The five per-group counts also sum to 728432 exactly.

(4) THE CAPS. The table's caps are 37 at an orbit of size 2, **22** at size 4, 17 at a size-8 cyclic orbit and 13 at a size-8 regular orbit of \(Z_2^3\) or \(Z_4 \times Z_2\). The 22 is the theorem I reviewed at height 5068, and using it rather than the raw orbit-lemma value of 26 is exactly right. Two observations. First, the table gives no cap for a size-8 regular orbit of \(D_4\) or \(Q_8\), which are two of the five groups counted; the row for those groups must be using some cap, and the artifact does not say which. Second, that gap is why I cannot reproduce the per-group counts: the enumeration is a sum over stabiliser multisets with \(c(G)\) bounded by the minimum cap over the used subgroups, so the missing entries are load-bearing for two of the five numbers. Stating them would make the row reproducible.

(5) THE SELF-CRITICISM, WHICH IS THE PASS'S MOST TRANSFERABLE PART. The note records that the first version used 26 at size-4 orbits — the raw lemma value — and so reported \(\max\lvert\mathrm{Fix}\rvert = 26\) for \(Z_4 \times Z_2\), and that this is the same failure the author had diagnosed in another lane two passes earlier: a lane's strongest result not pointed at a newly opened row because it is filed under the row it was proved for. That is an accurate diagnosis and it matches what I have seen from the outside in this fleet — it is the same shape as the order-57 episode, where a scoped conditionality was propagated because the superseding artifact was filed elsewhere. The remedy in both cases is an index, not a theorem.

(6) THE METHOD CHANGE. Replacing canonical forms with Burnside is the right move when the group acting on the objects is the large one — \(\lvert GL_3(2)\rvert = 168\) canonicalisations per accepted action is what stalled the direct enumeration — and it is verifiable in principle against the direct count on the four groups where that finished. The artifact reports exactly that cross-check structure, which is the correct way to trust a Burnside count.

(7) Scope. The row is a sizing, not an exclusion, and the note says so: it supplies the caps and the counts so that a successor can price the row rather than probe it. Its conclusion — that order 8 is not an escape from order 4 but about 530 times worse — follows from the counts it establishes, and it corrects an earlier reading based on a single probe.
