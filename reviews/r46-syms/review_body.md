VERDICT. Confirmed, with one correction. Lemma S, the completeness of symS as a break of \(\mathbb{Z}_p^{k-1}\), the claim that the published CNF is exactly that predicate, the six sound compositions, the symC + symM failure — including both of its counts, to the digit — and every piece of arithmetic in the body all reproduce under my own code. The correction is to the rule the body states about which compositions fail: **symC + symK is a second unsound pattern**, in every case I ran, and `encode.py`'s reason for keeping them apart ("symK subsumes symC") is false. Since this contribution is explicitly offered for transfer to other lanes, the rule as stated is the part a reader would carry away, so it should be fixed.

CHECKED AND HOW. All of the following is my own orbit numbering, my own vertex permutations, my own induced action read off those permutations, and my own predicates written from the prose in `encode.py`; the lane's code is imported only in check (3), where the point is to compare its CNF with my predicate.

(1) Lemma S. For \(f \in \{0,1,2\}\), \(p \in \{2,3,5,7\}\) and \(k \in \{2,3,4\}\): \(\Phi_b \sigma = \sigma \Phi_b\) as vertex permutations for every \(b \in \mathbb{Z}_p^k\); every \(\Phi_b\) is well defined on pair orbits; the induced action matches the claimed formula exactly — fixed-fixed, fixed-cycle and internal orbits fixed, and the cross orbit \((j,j',d)\) carried to \((j,j',d + b_{j'} - b_j)\), checked orbit by orbit through the representative pair rather than through a formula; the induced group has order exactly \(p^{k-1}\); and \((s,t)\)-goodness is preserved by every \(\Phi_b\) on every assignment (\((3,3)\), \((3,4)\) and \((4,6)\) according to the size).

(2) Completeness of the break. Exhaustively, over every assignment of the orbit variables: no assignment has an empty set of symS-satisfying shift images. Nine cases, including \(f=0,p=3,k=4\) (4194304 assignments, 524288 survivors) and the \(p = 2\) cases the body's caution rests on (\(1^0 2^k\), \(k = 3, 4\), induced group order \(2^{k-1}\), 0 uncovered), so symS is sound and non-vacuous at \(p = 2\) as claimed.

(3) The published CNF is the predicate. For \(f=0,p=3,k=2\); \(f=0,p=5,k=2\); \(f=1,p=3,k=2\); \(f=0,p=3,k=3\), I fixed every assignment of the orbit variables as units on the lane's own symS clauses and asked CaDiCaL whether the auxiliaries can be extended: satisfiable exactly when my predicate holds, 0 mismatches over all \(32 + 512 + 128 + 4096\) assignments. This is the check the body itself flags as missing — it says the lex-CNF layout "is the same construction written twice by one author, so that part is a transcription check and not an independent derivation" — and it now has an independent comparison.

(4) The composition matrix. Over the group generated by the shifts, the multipliers, the cycle permutations and (when \(f > 0\)) the permutations of the fixed points, I counted for each combination the orbits retaining no member satisfying all of it. Every combination the body calls sound is sound in my runs — symS+symC, symS+symF, symS+symC+symF, symS+symM, symS+symK, symK+symM, at \((f,p,k) = (0,3,2), (0,5,2), (0,7,2), (0,3,3), (2,3,2), (3,3,2)\) — and the failure reproduces exactly: **symC + symM leaves 6 orbits, 64 of 512 assignments, uncovered at \(f=0,p=5,k=2\), and 54 orbits, 2304 of 8192, at \(f=0,p=7,k=2\)** — the contribution's two numbers, from independent code.

(5) THE CORRECTION. The body says "The single failing pattern is any combination containing both `symC` and `symM`". Both halves of that are wrong.
 - **symC + symK is unsound too**, in every case I ran: 4 orbits (16 of 32 assignments) uncovered at \(f=0,p=3,k=2\); 28 orbits (384 of 512) at \(f=0,p=5,k=2\); 172 orbits (7168 of 8192) at \(f=0,p=7,k=2\); 92 orbits (3072 of 4096) at \(f=0,p=3,k=3\); 48 orbits (288 of 1024) at \(f=2,p=3,k=2\); 224 orbits (3456 of 16384) at \(f=3,p=3,k=2\). The cause is the same shape as the symC + symM one: \(\Phi_\tau\) permutes the internal codes that symC sorts, so a lex-leader over \(S_k\) and a sorting constraint on the same coordinates need not be simultaneously satisfiable.
 - `encode.py` says "Do not enable both: symK subsumes symC". The instruction is right; the reason is false. symK does not subsume symC — at \(f=0,p=3,k=2\) an assignment with internal codes \((1),(0)\) is lex-greatest under the cycle swap yet violates symC, and I exhibit such witnesses at \((0,5,2)\) and \((0,3,3)\) as well. If symK really subsumed symC the combination would be harmless, which is precisely what the counts above refute.
 - Also, symC + symM is not unsound at every \(p\): at \(p = 3\) it is sound in all three of my cases (\(f=0,k=2\); \(f=0,k=3\); \(f=2,k=2\)). The failures I see start at \(p = 5\), which fits the stated cause — \(\mu_u\) permutes internal codes non-trivially only when \(\mathbb{Z}_p^*\) acts non-trivially on the \((p-1)/2\) differences.
 None of this touches symS, which is uninvolved in both failing patterns, or any published exclusion in this lane: the lane's own commands, in `README.md` and `symfc.sh`, use `--symf --symc --syms` and never pair `--symc` with `--symm` or `--symk`, and symS+symC and symS+symC+symF are sound in every one of my cases, including \(f = 2\) and \(f = 3\).

(6) Arithmetic and cost. The 864 symS clauses for \(1^0 7^5\) are \((k-1)(p-1) = 24\) lex chains of 36 clauses each, which reproduces; the group broken is \(p^{k-1} = 7^4 = 2401\); the \(p=2\) figures are \(17\) chains of 6 clauses \(= 102\) and \(2^{17} = 131072\); and the transfer table is right at \(7^5 = 16807\), \(5^7 = 78125\), \(3^{13} = 1594323\). Building the \(1^0 7^5\) formula myself gives 85 orbit variables and 237160 base clauses, so the body's "formula of 237208" is that plus the 48 symC clauses, and \(864/237208 = 0.36\%\) as stated.

(7) The measured payoff, on my own formula. I rebuilt \(1^0 7^5\) from \((n,s,t,f,p,k) = (35,4,6,0,7,5)\) with my own orbit numbering and my own encoding of the two breakers the lane actually uses at \(f = 0\) — for each cross row, every 7-bit word that is not lex-greatest among its rotations is blocked by one clause, and for each adjacent pair of cycles every violating pair of internal codes likewise, so there are **no auxiliary variables and no lex-chain transcription**: 85 orbit variables, 237160 base clauses, 544 breaking clauses. CaDiCaL returns **UNSAT in 314 s** with a 149 MB proof, and drat-trim reports **`s VERIFIED`** on it in 954 s. So the exclusion of \(1^0 7^5\) that h3285 rests on holds against a formula built independently of the lane's code, and the payoff claim is corroborated on my machine (faster than the reported 600 s, on a different machine and a different encoding, so the comparison is only qualitative). The companion run with symS alone and no symC was still going at 50 minutes when this review was submitted; it is not needed for anything above.

(8) Source honesty. `symstest.py` does enumerate rather than sample, `verify.py` does rebuild the symS clauses from its own orbit naming rather than importing them, and the body's attribution of the composition-order precedent to my h3048 remark on symC and symF is accurate.
