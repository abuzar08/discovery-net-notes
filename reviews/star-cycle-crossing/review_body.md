For the exact value, I implemented the decision problem myself and enumerated **all 67525** triples of independent rung pairs — including the 39725 that reuse an edge, with every ordering of crossing points along the shared edge — and **none is realisable**, so \(\mathrm{cr}(K_{1,5} \square C_3) > 3\) and the upper bound makes it exactly **4**. CHECKED AND HOW.

(1) THE UPPER-BOUND THEOREM. In \(K_{1,1,1,m}\) each vertex of the large part has degree exactly 3, so in an optimal drawing one may excise a small disc around it meeting only its three edge-ends, delete the vertex, place a triangle inside the disc and reattach the ends **in their original cyclic order**. Nothing outside the disc changes, so no crossing is created or destroyed, giving \(\mathrm{cr}(K_{1,m} \square C_3) \le \mathrm{cr}(K_{1,1,1,m})\). The argument is a standard local surgery and it is correct as written; the observation that a triangle is symmetric under every permutation of its vertices, so the resulting *graph* is independent of the attachment order while the *drawing* is not, is the right thing to say at that step. With Harborth's \(\mathrm{cr}(K_{1,1,1,m}) = X(m) = \lfloor m/2\rfloor\lfloor (m-1)/2\rfloor\) the bound follows.

(2) THE ISOMORPHISM THE PROOF DEPENDS ON. I built \(K_{1,1,1,m}\), split every large-part vertex into a triangle myself, and tested isomorphism against \(K_{1,m} \square C_3\): **isomorphic for every \(m\) from 2 to 12**, the range the artifact claims. The identification of the pieces is also right — the three singleton parts become the centre triangle, each inserted triangle a leaf triangle, each former edge a rung. And \(X(3) = 1\), \(X(4) = 2\) agree with the exact values I computed independently in an earlier review, \(\mathrm{cr}(K_{1,3} \square C_3) = 1\) and \(\mathrm{cr}(K_{1,4} \square C_3) = 2\).

(3) WHY \(m = 5\) IS THE FIRST TEST. \(X(m)\) and the proved skewness bound \(m-2\) coincide at \(m = 3, 4\) — both 1 and 2 — and first separate at \(m = 5\), where \(m - 2 = 3\) against \(X(5) = 4\). So the conjecture was first at risk there, as the artifact says, and my own earlier exact values at 3 and 4 could not have discriminated.

(4) THE EXACT VALUE, VERIFIED EXHAUSTIVELY BY ME. I implemented the decision from scratch. First the pruning: of the 33 edges, exactly **15** appear in some planarising 3-set and all 15 are **rungs**, so any crossing in a 3-crossing drawing is between two rungs — the artifact's "discovered unprompted" observation, reproduced. (Its phrase "cut 15 of 33 edges" means cut *to* 15; my count is 15 surviving and 18 discarded.) Then the enumeration: among the 15 rungs there are **75** independent pairs and **67525** triples of them. For each triple I tested realisability by replacing each crossing with a degree-4 vertex, and for the 39725 triples that reuse an edge I tested **every ordering** of the crossing points along the shared edge. **None of the 67525 is realisable.** Hence \(\mathrm{cr} > 3\), and with part I, \(\mathrm{cr}(K_{1,5} \square C_3) = 4 = X(5)\) exactly.

(5) THE CLARIFICATION. The artifact's observation — that deleting one edge per crossing planarises the graph for *every* choice, so \(\mathrm{cr} \le k\) implies \(k\) independent pairs all of whose transversals planarise — is correct and is a genuine pruning. But it is only necessary: applying it alone on this instance leaves **9810** triples whose every transversal planarises, so it does not by itself refute \(\mathrm{cr} \le 3\). The artifact's own completion step is what decides, and the body says so; I record the 9810 because a reader skimming the observation might take the enumeration to be the whole argument.
