(4) The König table, mine and validated. I validated my constrained maximum by brute force over **all** bipartite graphs with parts of sizes \((3,3)\), \((3,4)\) and \((4,4)\) and minimum degree 2 on the \(Z\) side: in every case the true maximum of \(\sum_z a_z\) among graphs of matching number at most \(k\) equals my bound, so the bound is exact, not merely valid.

(6) The triangles and the clique cover. \(\mu_1 + \mu_2 \ge 12\) and \(\lvert Z\rvert = 7\) give at least 5 vertices of \(Z\) saturated on both sides; their \(Q_1\)- and \(Q_2\)-representatives are distinct and \(Q_1, Q_2, Z\) are disjoint, so the \(t\) triangles are vertex-disjoint, and each is a triangle of \(H\) because there are no \(G\)-edges between the blocks. Removing them leaves \(K_{24-t,24-t}\) inside \(H[L]\), covered by exactly \(24 - t\) cliques, so \(\theta(H) \le t + (24 - t) + \theta(H[R'])\). At \(m = 827\), \(t = 4\) and the surviving \(H\)-edge gives \(\theta(H) \le 4 + 20 + 4 = 28\); at \(m = 828\), \(t = 5\) gives \(5 + 19 + 4 = 28\). Both contradict \(\theta(H) = \chi(G) = 29\). I checked that the three parts cover all 57 vertices, \(3t + 2(24-t) + (9-t) = 57\), and that Fact 1 is load-bearing exactly where the body puts it: at \(m = 827\) the single \(H[R]\)-edge must survive the absorption, which is guaranteed only because one endpoint is a \(w_i\) and \(w_i\) is never absorbed — if both endpoints lay in \(Z\) and were absorbed, \(\theta(H[R'])\) would be 5 and the bound would give exactly 29, no contradiction.
