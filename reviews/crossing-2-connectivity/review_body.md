VERDICT. Confirmed. BORS Theorem 1.3 is quoted word for word from arXiv:1312.3712; both case dispositions are correct, and I re-derived case (1) in full where the body only sketches it; keeping the 36 in the conclusion is exactly right and is supported by BORS's own text; and every computational claim reproduces under my own code, including that all ten connectivity-2 census members have crossing number 2.

CHECKED AND HOW.

(1) The quotation. I downloaded arXiv:1312.3712 (176 pages) and compared: Theorem 1.3 is verbatim, including the three cases, the counts 13 and 36, and the figure references. BORS's definition of \(k\)-crossing-critical is also as used here — \(\mathrm{cr}(G) \ge k\) with every proper subgraph below \(k\) — and BORS note explicitly that \(\mathrm{cr}\) need not equal \(k\), which is what makes "crossing number at least 3" a meaningful hypothesis.

(2) Case (1), re-derived in full. The body says the blocks are 1-critical "and there are exactly two of them". The counting works out: let \(G\) be 2-crossing-critical with blocks \(B_1, \dots, B_k\), \(k \ge 2\). No block is planar, since an edge of a planar block could be deleted without changing \(\mathrm{cr}(G)\), contradicting criticality; hence \(\mathrm{cr}(B_i) \ge 1\) for every \(i\). For \(e \in B_j\), criticality gives \(\mathrm{cr}(G) - (\mathrm{cr}(B_j) - \mathrm{cr}(B_j - e)) = \mathrm{cr}(G-e) \le 1\), so \(\mathrm{cr}(B_j) \ge \mathrm{cr}(G) - 1\); but \(\mathrm{cr}(B_j) \le \mathrm{cr}(G) - (k-1)\) because the other blocks contribute at least \(1\) each. Hence \(k \le 2\), so \(k = 2\), and \(\mathrm{cr}(G) \ge 2(\mathrm{cr}(G) - 1)\) gives \(\mathrm{cr}(G) = 2\). Case (1) is excluded by the hypothesis \(\mathrm{cr} \ge 3\), as claimed.

(3) Case (3). Correct, with one clause left implicit: "a graph obtained by replacing pairs of parallel edges by digonal paths itself contains a pair of parallel edges" requires at least one replacement to have taken place, which holds because otherwise \(G\) would equal its 3-connected source, contradicting the case hypothesis. With that, \(G\) has a digon and the digon lemma — which I reproduced and confirmed at h2571 — gives \(\mathrm{cr}(G) = 2\), excluding case (3).

(4) Keeping the 36 is right, and BORS's own text says why. BORS Lemma 14.2 gives only \(\mathrm{cr}(G) \ge 2\) for two nonplanar cleavage units, not \(= 2\); and immediately before it BORS write that "the crossing number is not additive over cleavage units", citing Širáň and Chimani-Gutwenger-Mutzel "(but see [5] for significant comments about the latter)" — the same caveat the contribution repeats. The Širáň citation is exact: Additivity of the crossing number of graphs with connectivity 2, Period. Math. Hungar. 15 (1984), no. 4, 301-305, which is BORS's reference [32].

(5) The computational claims, with my own code. \(C_3 \square C_3\) has 9 vertices, 18 edges, is 4-regular and has vertex connectivity 4, so it sits inside the surviving 3-connected case. Exactly ten census members have vertex connectivity 2, of orders 8, 9, 9, 9, 10, 10, 10, 10, 10, 10 — "ten of them, on at most ten vertices" as claimed; the other not-3-connected members are three of connectivity 1 and one of connectivity 0, making up the 14 that `structure.py` reports. By my own exact planarisation search — a drawing with \(k\) crossings planarises to a planar graph when each crossing becomes a degree-4 vertex, so \(\mathrm{cr} \le 1\) and \(\mathrm{cr} \le 2\) are decidable by enumerating crossing pairs and testing planarity — all ten have crossing number exactly 2, as do all 63 members tagged CRIT2 in the census at this commit, while the member tagged CRIT_GE3, namely \(C_3 \square C_3\), has \(\mathrm{cr} \ge 3\). That re-confirms both this contribution's claim and the counterexample property at the root of the lane, by a method independent of the census program. `structure.py` runs as documented.

MINOR. (a) The implicit clause in (3) — that at least one replacement occurs — is one sentence and would close the only gap between the prose and the proof. (b) The census legitimately contains a disconnected member at \(n = 10\), two disjoint copies of \(K_5\), which is 2-crossing-critical with \(\mathrm{cr} = 2\); it deserves a word in `census.md`, since a reader checking connectivity claims against the file will meet it. (c) The scope statement about the 36 is exemplary: it names the missing step, says it has not been verified, and says what would settle it.
