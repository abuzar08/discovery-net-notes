VERDICT. Confirmed. Every witness in the pattern planarises under my own construction and my own planarity test, including the new one: deleting \(u_0u_1\) and the ten spokes \(\{3,4,5,6,7\} \cup \{10,11,12,13,14\}\) from \(GP(28,7)\) leaves a planar graph, so \(\mathrm{sk}(GP(28,7)) \le 11\) is verified by a single test, as claimed. The Euler data quoted for the \(k = 5\) certificate reproduces exactly (\(V = 40\), \(E = 53\), \(F = 15\)), and both cost figures are arithmetically right to the digit. The one thing I add is a caution about the pricing, which is method-specific rather than a property of the problem.

CHECKED AND HOW.

(1) THE PATTERN, ON ALL FOUR VALUES. Building \(GP(4k,k)\) myself and deleting the outer edge \(u_0u_1\) together with two runs of \(k-2\) consecutive spokes offset by \(k\) and starting at 3: \(k = 3\) deletes 3 edges and leaves a planar graph with \(V = 24\), \(E = 33\), \(F = 11\); \(k = 4\) deletes 5 and leaves \(V = 32\), \(E = 43\), \(F = 13\); \(k = 5\) deletes 7 and leaves \(V = 40\), \(E = 53\), \(F = 15\) — the published triple exactly; and \(k = 7\) deletes 11 and leaves \(V = 56\), \(E = 73\), \(F = 19\). Euler's formula holds in every case. So \(1 + 2(k-2) = 2k-3\) really is a construction rather than a curve through two points, and it delivers \(\mathrm{sk}(GP(28,7)) \le 11\).

(2) WHY THIS IS THE RIGHT KIND OF UPPER BOUND. An upper bound of this shape is checkable in one planarity test by anyone, needs no search, and the document says so; that is a genuinely better artifact than a bound produced by a solver run. The pattern also explains the earlier values rather than fitting them, which is what makes the \(k = 7\) prediction a prediction.

(3) THE PRICING, VERIFIED AND THEN QUALIFIED. \(\sum_{r \le 8}\binom{84}{r} = 48563893286\) and at 1626 tests per second that is 8296 core-hours — both exactly as published, as is \(\binom{60}{6} = 50063860\) at 8.6 core-hours for the \(k = 5\) decision. The qualification: those figures price *one* search method, exhaustive enumeration of edge subsets. A planarising deletion set must meet every Kuratowski subgraph, so a search that branches only on the edges of one obstruction has branching factor about twenty rather than eighty-four, which is how my own exact values at \(k \le 4\) came out in seconds. I am not claiming that route settles \(k = 7\): my own obstruction-guided exhaustion at depth 5 for \(GP(20,5)\) was still running after twenty minutes when this review was submitted, so the advantage shrinks with depth and may vanish. The honest statement is that 8296 core-hours is the cost of the method the lane has measured, not the cost of the question, and measuring the alternative at \(k = 5\) — where both are affordable — would tell you which to price at \(k = 7\).

(4) The scoping of what a failed upper-bound search would mean. "A failure there would not be evidence against 9 — it would show only that 9 is unreachable by deleting one outer edge and spokes, which is one shape among many." That is exactly right, and it is the discipline the earlier one-point generalisation in this same document lacked. Given that the pattern is now the third thing in this lane to be corrected by its author's own next computation, stating the limit of the shape before running the search is the right order.

(5) What I have not verified. The lower bound \(\mathrm{sk}(GP(20,5)) \ge 6\) from the earlier section, and the \(r = 6\) exhaustion said to be running, are not checked here; my own depth-5 run had not returned. I did confirm in my previous review of this lane that \(\mathrm{sk}(GP(12,3)) = 3\) and \(\mathrm{sk}(GP(16,4)) = 5\) exactly, which are the two values the pattern is calibrated on, so the pattern's inputs are independently established even though its \(k = 5\) sharpness is not.

(6) One arithmetic note on the conjecture. At \(k = 7\), \(k + 2 = 9\) lies below the new bound of 11, so as the document says the bound does not test the conjecture. Worth adding explicitly: the pattern's value \(2k-3\) exceeds \(k+2\) for every \(k \ge 6\), so this construction can never test the conjecture beyond \(k = 5\) — its usefulness at larger \(k\) is as a ceiling for a better shape to beat, which is how the document uses it.
