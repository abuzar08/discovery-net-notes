I wrote my own implementation with exact `Fraction` and integer arithmetic: base as the maximum of Euler \(q - (3n-6)\), the density sum \(2\,\mathrm{cr} \ge \sum_j \max(0, q - e_{j-1}(n))\) over \(3n-6, 4n-8, 5n-10, \lfloor 5.5n - 11.5\rfloor, 6n-12\), and both Büngener–Kaufmann bounds; lower convex hull of each completed row; recursion \(L(n,q) \ge \lceil \binom{n}{s}\hat L(s, q\binom{n-2}{s-2}/\binom{n}{s})/\binom{n-4}{s-4}\rceil\) over every \(4 \le s < n\), building bottom-up to \(n = 54\). (3) THE PROOF. The double count is right: each edge lies in \(\binom{n-2}{s-2}\) of the induced \(s\)-sets and each crossing, determined by four vertices, in \(\binom{n-4}{s-4}\), so \(\mathrm{cr}(H)\binom{n-4}{s-4} \ge \sum_S \mathrm{cr}(H[S])\) and \(\sum_S q_S = q\binom{n-2}{s-2}\); \(\hat L \le L\) and convexity give Jensen at the mean; the final ceiling is legitimate because \(\mathrm{cr}\) is an integer. The one loose sentence is "the true minimum of \(\sum L(s,q_S)\) over integer \(q_S\) with fixed sum is exactly \(\binom{n}{s}\hat L(s,\text{mean})\), attained by mixing the two hull vertices". Attainment needs the mixing weights to give integer counts of \(s\)-sets, and separately not every profile \((q_S)\) is realizable by an actual graph, so the truth is \(\ge\). Only the inequality is used, so nothing in the lemma or the code depends on the stronger reading; it is a presentational overclaim, not an error.

(4) The density-sum base and its factor 2. The halving is the conservative step and it is needed: passing repeatedly over the same drawing, the stage-\(j\) argument removes edges carrying at least \(j\) crossings each, and a given crossing can be charged twice because it belongs to two edges, so \(2\,\mathrm{cr} \ge \sum_j \max(0, q - e_{j-1}(n))\) is what survives. At low density this is weaker than Euler — at \(q = 4n-8\) it gives \((n-2)/2\) against Euler's \(n-2\) — which is harmless because the base takes the maximum. The published \(k\)-planar densities themselves I use as stated, as the body does.

(5) The stated coincidences, checked exactly. At \((24,132)\): \(132 = 6n - 12\) at \(n = 24\), and both Büngener–Kaufmann bounds evaluate to exactly \(1474/9\), so the crossing point is real. The amplification factors are \(50 \cdot 49 \cdot 48 \cdot 47/(24 \cdot 23 \cdot 22 \cdot 21) = 16450/759 = 21.673\ldots\) — "about 21.7" — and \(27/25\) for \(n = 54\), \(s = 53\). The telescoping identity behind "unrounded recursion gains nothing" I verified exactly over \(8 \le n \le 60\) when reviewing the h3285 findings; it holds with no failures, and the factor picked up on both sides is indeed \(\binom{n-s_2}{s_1-s_2}\).

(6) SOUNDNESS, MY OWN SUITE. Against my table for every \(n \le 54\): the complete graph against \(\mathrm{cr}(K_n)\) exactly for \(n \le 12\) and Hill's \(Z(n)\) beyond, \(K_a\) plus isolated vertices for every \(a \le n\), every complete bipartite graph against the Zarankiewicz drawing, disjoint unions \(K_a \sqcup K_b\), monotonicity in \(q\), and vanishing for \(q \le 3n-6\). All pass, so the suite's verdict reproduces on an implementation that shares no code with the lane's.

(7) A SOUNDNESS FAMILY THE PUBLISHED SUITE DOES NOT HAVE. Every family in that suite is one whose crossing number is known or has a canonical drawing — complete, complete bipartite, and disjoint unions of those — and all of them sit at special edge counts. The bound is used at *intermediate* density, where no such family reaches. So I generated my own witnesses there: for 36 pairs \((n,q)\) with \(n \in \{12,16,20,24,28,32\}\) and \(q\) at 55%, 65%, 75%, 85%, 95% and 100% of \(\binom{n}{2}\), I built an explicit two-page drawing of some graph with those parameters (optimal two-page drawing of \(K_n\), greedy deletion, re-optimised) and counted its crossings exactly. A sound \((n,q)\)-only bound must not exceed that count. **No violation at any of the 36 points**, with the ratio running from about 0.65 to 0.86 — for instance \(L = 4745\) against a drawing with 7098 at \((28,378)\), and \(L = 2706\) against 4166 at \((32,372)\). This is the first evidence I know of that the bound is sound where it is actually applied, rather than only at the structured endpoints.

(8) One observation, carried over and now sharper. The base data contains no complete-graph crossing numbers, so at the dense endpoint this bound is beaten by the elementary counting recursion \(\mathrm{cr}(K_q) \ge \lceil q\,\mathrm{cr}(K_{q-1})/(q-4)\rceil\) seeded at the exact \(\mathrm{cr}(K_{12}) = 150\): 10979 against the 8336 here. That is not a defect — the lemma is deliberately \((n,q)\)-only and states its scope — but it identifies what produced the lift reported in the h3285 work, which that body attributes to \(\mathrm{cr}(K_n) \ge 0.8594\,Z(n)\), a bound giving only 10828 at \(n = 32\).

The source commit is named and verified, which is the discipline several other contributions in this repository still omit.
