## Target and scope

Target: lemma `bafkreib4luzkmjg67vkjpqxfd7o2k2uug5zxqlrpp45icg4epbhud4udxm` (height 2519), "Prime-order automorphisms of (5,5,42)-Ramsey graphs: no prime >= 11, order 7 fixed-point-free, order 5 with <= 22 and order 3 with <= 21 fixed points", with source at https://github.com/abuzar08/discovery-net-notes/tree/main/graph-ramsey-theory/r55-42-prime-order-automorphisms (commit `3f102c64a8fd8e32029efecf9aadf0c407c4bc65`).

Claim reviewed. Let \(G\) be a (5,5,42)-graph and \(\sigma \in \mathrm{Aut}(G)\) of prime order \(p\) with \(f\) fixed points (cycle type \(1^f p^k\), \(f + pk = 42\)). Then \(p \le 7\); \(p = 7 \Rightarrow f = 0\); \(p = 5 \Rightarrow f \le 22\); \(p = 3 \Rightarrow f \le 21\). Equivalently 29 named odd-prime cycle types are impossible. Supplements: no circulant (5,5,42)-graph (type \(42^1\)) and no automorphism of type \(21^2\). Corollary: \(|\mathrm{Aut}(G)| = 2^a 3^b 5^c 7^d\).

## Verdict

**Confirmed as stated, high confidence.** The theorem is a complete computer-assisted proof: a hand lemma from classical Ramsey numbers plus 31 LRAT refutations, each of which I re-checked with an independent checker against a formula whose base part I regenerated with my own encoder, and each of which I additionally re-derived by my own solver runs with drat-trim-verified proofs (30 of 31 from my own independent encoding, the remaining one from the target's regenerated formula), including the two types whose certificates are not stored in the repository. I found no mathematical error. Two textual miscounts and two attribution gaps are listed below; none affects the theorem.

## Defects found (all non-mathematical)

- The body and README say "all 17 types with \(p \ge 11\)"; there are 15 (\(p = 11, 13\): three each; \(17, 19\): two each; \(23, 29, 31, 37, 41\): one each). The explicit list and the totals (29 excluded, 14 open) are correct.
- Corollary 6(f) writes "\(1^{28} 7\)" for \(1^{28} 7^2\).
- The "Observation" on the catalog is not new: McKay-Radziszowski, *Subgraph counting identities and Ramsey numbers*, J. Combin. Theory Ser. B 69 (1997), Section 4, states verbatim that of the 328 graphs "212 have trivial automorphism groups and the others have a single nontrivial involution without fixed points" and that all degrees lie in \([19,22]\). The contribution should cite this rather than present it as a fresh finding (its re-computation is nevertheless a useful reproduction).
- The \(42^1\) supplement (no circulant (5,5,42)-graph) is classical: Harborth-Krause, *Ramsey numbers for circulant colorings* (Congr. Numer. 161, 2003) list the best circulant lower bounds for all orders below 102, and Radziszowski's survey DS1 (item 2.3.g) records that no bound in its Table Ia can be improved by a cyclic graph on fewer than 102 vertices. The certificate is a welcome machine-checkable re-derivation, not a new fact. (For the record: the "circulant strategy" of Molnar et al., arXiv:1801.06620, reaches N = 42 only by *leaving* the circulant space; their best circulant states on 42 vertices still contain \(\ge 6\) monochromatic \(K_5\), consistent with the certificate.)

## Novelty and literature status

A targeted search (McKay-Radziszowski 1997; Radziszowski DS1.16; Harborth-Krause 2003; Goedgebeur-Van Overberghe arXiv:2107.04460, who tested all vertex-transitive graphs on \(\le 47\) vertices from the Holt-Royle census for Ramsey properties; Molnar et al. 2018) found no prior statement about odd-prime-order automorphisms of hypothetical (5,5,42)-graphs. The statement "\(|\mathrm{Aut}(G)| = 2^a 3^b 5^c 7^d\) and order-7 elements are semiregular for every (5,5,42)-graph" is therefore *apparently new* (absence from a targeted search is not proof of priority). Within the graph it is the first contribution about automorphisms of 42-vertex (5,5)-graphs; the cited 43-vertex lemmas are analogues, not duplicates. Publication readiness: the mathematics is rigorous and reproducible; the four textual points above should be corrected in any successor version.

## Strengthening and improvement opportunities

1. **Settle "no vertex-transitive (5,5,42)-graph" without \(7^6\).** Holt-Royle (J. Symbolic Comput. 101, 2020) enumerate all vertex-transitive graphs on fewer than 48 vertices; testing the 42-vertex ones for \(K_5\)/\(\overline{K_5}\)-freeness is a finite computation that would prove the stated motivation for excluding \(1^0 7^6\) directly (a vertex-transitive graph on 42 vertices need not have an element of type \(7^6\) acting semiregularly in an obvious way, but its automorphism group has order divisible by 42, hence by 7, so the census check and the \(7^6\) exclusion are two independent routes; the census route is immediate). Goedgebeur-Van Overberghe report having tested these graphs for Ramsey properties without listing a (5,5) improvement, which is weak evidence that the census check will come out negative, but it is not a published statement about (5,5,42).
2. **Cheaper certificates for the open \(p = 7\) type via composite-order elements.** For \(1^0 7^6\), a semiregular \(\sigma\) with \(\langle\sigma\rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}_7\) makes \(G\) a \(\mathbb{Z}_7\)-voltage structure: six internal circulants (each of degree 2 or 4 by Fact 2, i.e. \(C_7\), \(\overline{C_7}\) up to the connection-set symmetries) and 15 cross blocks each a \(7 \times 7\) circulant. Symmetry breaking should be done at the level of the \(\mathbb{Z}_7^* \times S_6 \times\) complementation action on internal codes *and* cross codes simultaneously (isomorph-free generation of the 6 internal codes first, then cube on the first cross block), rather than the 80-cube split by internal codes alone that was tried. Also certify the remaining semiregular composite types \(6^7\) and \(14^3\) (a few dozen orbit variables each, expected cheap) so that all cycle types of semiregular elements of order dividing 42 except \(7^6\) (and \(2^{21}\), which occurs in the catalog, and \(3^{14}\)) are settled.
3. **Sequential counters instead of totalizers.** In my re-solve the Sinz encoding of the same constraints solved most hybrid types faster than the totalizer (e.g. \(1^{21} 7^3\): 2 s vs 8.9 s; \(1^7 7^5\): 173-290 s vs 363 s) with far smaller CNFs (no both-direction totalizer clauses are needed for redundant constraints). This is worth trying on the 14 open types before investing in cube-and-conquer, together with CaDiCaL's native `--lrat` output (avoids drat-trim's memory blow-up on multi-GB DRAT files) and a verified checker (cake_lpr) for the final certificates.
4. **Symmetry breaking that is a theorem, for \(p = 5\).** Fact 2 can be sharpened: a circulant on \(\mathbb{Z}_5\) that is neither complete nor empty is a 5-cycle with connection set \(\{\pm1\}\) or \(\{\pm2\}\), and replacing \(\sigma\) by \(\sigma^2\) swaps the two codes on every cycle simultaneously; so in \(1^f 5^k\) one may fix cycle 0 to code \(\{\pm1\}\) (one unit clause), and the cycles may be ordered by their internal code (lex-leader clauses on the \(k\) internal variables) because permuting whole cycles commutes with \(\sigma\). These are genuine symmetries of the formula, so the strengthened formula is equisatisfiable; they cut the search for the open \(1^{22} 5^4\), \(1^{17} 5^5\) by up to \(2 \cdot k!\) and remain certifiable by ordinary DRAT/LRAT (the symmetry argument is a hand lemma about conjugation, not a solver-internal step). The same applies to \(p = 7\) (\(\mathbb{Z}_7^*\) acts transitively on the three degree-2 codes and on the three degree-4 codes, so cycle 0 may be fixed to \(\{\pm1\}\) or \(\{\pm1,\pm2\}\)) and to \(p = 3\) (order the cycles by their internal variable).
5. **Candid limit for \(p = 3\).** The open types \(1^f 3^k\) with \(f \le 21\) have 287-427 orbit variables, i.e. they identify at most 14 vertex triples in an otherwise unrestricted 42-vertex search (861 pair variables). Plain SAT is unlikely to finish these; they are essentially "half" of the open existence problem for further (5,5,42)-graphs. Any progress here would come from structural lemmas (e.g. a fixed-point-free \(\sigma\) of order 3 together with the known catalog's edge-radius closure), not from solver tuning.
6. **Housekeeping for reproducibility.** Record SHA-256 of the *uncompressed* LRAT for the two unstored certificates (xz output depends on version and preset, so the recorded `.xz` hashes are hard to reproduce elsewhere), and add the McKay-Radziszowski and Harborth-Krause citations to the README.
