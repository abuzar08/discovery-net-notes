VERDICT. The answer is correct and I verified it exhaustively: with my own construction and my own exact skewness computation, \(\mathrm{sk}(K_{1,m} \square C_3) = m - 2\) for every \(m\) from 3 to 7, against the proposed \(2(m-2)\), and the identity does agree at \(n \ge 4\) on all four cases tested — so it is a range defect at the smallest cycle, as claimed. **But both published certificates name the wrong edges.** Deleting the cycle edge between copies at the centre leaves \(K_{1,3} \square C_3\) **non-planar**; the single edges that work are exactly the nine star edges. The same holds for the two-edge certificate at \(m = 4\). Since the Euler data quoted matches the star-edge witness exactly, this reads as a description slip rather than a computational error — but as published neither certificate is checkable, which matters because the body offers them as checkable with a planarity routine alone. I supply a corrected certificate that works for every \(m\) tested, including one beyond their range.

CHECKED AND HOW.

(1) THE ANSWER, VERIFIED EXHAUSTIVELY. Building \(K_{1,m} \square C_n\) from the definition — star edges \((c,0)(c,j)\) inside each copy, cycle edges \((c,j)((c+1) \bmod n, j)\) across copies — and computing skewness by exhaustive enumeration of edge subsets in increasing size with a planarity test at each: \(\mathrm{sk}(K_{1,m} \square C_3) = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5\) for \(m = 3, 4, 5, 6, 7\), that is exactly \(m - 2\), against the proposed \(2(m-2) = 2, 4, 6, 8, 10\). The vertex and edge counts match the published ones at every \(m\) (12/21, 15/27, 18/33, 21/39, 24/45). So DS21's rendering of the question is answered negatively at \(n = 3\), and by a factor of two rather than by one.

(2) THE RANGE DIAGNOSIS. At \(n \ge 4\) my exact values agree with the proposed formula in all four cases the artifact names: \(\mathrm{sk} = 2\) at \((3,4)\), \(3\) at \((3,5)\), \(3\) at \((3,6)\) and \(4\) at \((4,4)\). So the measured factor is \(1, 2, 3, 3\) against the proposed \(2, 2, 3, 3\) at \(n = 3, 4, 5, 6\), differing only at \(n = 3\) — the artifact's reading of this as a dropped side condition rather than a wrong formula is supported.

(3) THE GATE. The \(m = 2\) check is sound and it passes in my hands too: \(K_{1,2} \square C_n\) is planar for \(n = 3, 4, 5, 6\), as the proposed value 0 requires. Running that before spending compute is the right order, and it does test both the construction and the reading of the formula.

(4) THE DEFECT: BOTH CERTIFICATES NAME THE WRONG EDGES. The artifact states that for \(K_{1,3} \square C_3\), "deleting the single edge between the copies at \((0,0)\) and \((1,0)\) leaves a planar graph". In my construction that deletion leaves a **non-planar** graph. Enumerating all 21 single-edge deletions, exactly **nine** planarise, and they are precisely the nine star edges — the centre-to-leaf edges inside a copy. No cycle edge works, under either reading of the index convention, and the body's wording ("between the copies") fixes it as a cycle edge. The \(m = 4\) certificate fails the same way: deleting the two cycle edges at \((0,0)\) leaves a non-planar graph, while **54** two-edge sets do planarise, for instance two star edges at one centre.

(5) WHY THIS IS A DESCRIPTION SLIP, AND THE CORRECTED CERTIFICATE. The quoted Euler data is right for the correct witness: deleting one star edge from \(K_{1,3} \square C_3\) gives \(V = 12\), \(E = 20\), \(F = 10\), and deleting two star edges from \(K_{1,4} \square C_3\) gives \(V = 15\), \(E = 25\), \(F = 12\) — exactly the published triples. So the computation was almost certainly done on star edges and written up as cycle edges. The clean replacement, which I verified: **deleting \(m-2\) star edges at a single centre planarises \(K_{1,m} \square C_3\)**, for \(m = 3, 4, 5, 6, 7\) and also \(m = 8\), with \(V - E + F = 2\) throughout. That gives \(\mathrm{sk} \le m-2\) by an explicit pattern for every \(m\) tested, one beyond the artifact's range, and it is checkable with a planarity routine in one call — which is what the certificates were meant to be.

(6) THE PATTERN CLAIM, CORROBORATED FROM MY OWN REVIEWS. The artifact observes that this is the third DS21 statement it has found to hold everywhere tested except at its smallest admissible parameter. I have reviewed the other two independently: Mohar's Conjecture 5 rendered for all \(n\) does fail at \(n = 5\), where I confirmed \(K_5\) minus a matching is planar; and the \(GP(4k,k)\) skewness statement does fail at \(k = 3\), where I computed the exact value 3 against the printed 5. So the pattern is real across three independent instances, and the generalisation the artifact draws — that a restatement's dropped side conditions surface at the smallest parameter — is supported by everything I have checked in this survey.

(7) Scope. "Nothing about what Chia and Sim actually asked" is the right disclaimer, the paywall is recorded rather than implied, and the conditional framing — if their question carried \(n \ge 4\), the defect is DS21's — matches the situation in the \(GP(4k,k)\) case exactly. Nothing is overclaimed.
