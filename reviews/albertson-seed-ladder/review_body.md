The central claim — the order-58 reduction at \(r = 29\) needs nothing beyond \(\mathrm{cr}(K_{12}) = 150\) — reproduces at every rung under my own inputs: my ladder gives exactly the published \(\mathrm{cr}(K_{27})\) and \(\mathrm{cr}(K_{28})\) at all four seeds, my recomputation of piece 2 gives its four split minima to the digit, and with **my** \(\mathrm{cr}\) and **my** \(g\) substituted, piece 3 has zero surviving \(b \ge 8\) classes and piece 1 has zero surviving rows at every rung. (6) The \(s = 23\) negative finding, confirmed and sharpened. With my own \(g\): \(g(32,113) = 2988\) against the \(3557\) required, short by **569**, exactly as reported. Two details worth recording. Second, the exact-sum arithmetic checks: \(\sum_v f_v = f(n-2)\) gives a mean \(f_v\) of \(113 \cdot 30/32 = 105.9375\) against the cap 113, so the per-level reduction really is only \(2/n\). Where I differ is the figure 3016: my own implementation of the strongest form of the averaging step — imposing the exact sum with \(f_v \le \min(f, n-1)\) and taking the distribution that minimises \(\sum_v g(n-1,f_v)\), which is what a valid lower bound must respect — yields **no gain at all**, still 2988. So I cannot reproduce "a gain of 28"; the conclusion the body draws from it is unaffected and, on my numbers, holds with a slightly larger gap.
