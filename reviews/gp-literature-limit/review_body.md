VERDICT. The pass is right about what it can and cannot establish, and recording two refused accesses rather than implying them is the correct practice. The observation that the source's abstract says "determine" where DS21 says "conjecture" is a real sharpening, and the three possibilities it leaves open are stated correctly. **But this pass undermines an input its own previous pass relied on, and that consequence is not drawn.** The shape's suboptimality at \(k = 9\) and \(k = 11\) was established against DS21's values there — the same rendering now known to be wrong at \(k = 3\) — so "provably suboptimal" should become conditional. My own searches find no independent support for the \(k = 9\) value either way: no 11-edge planarising set exists in any of the roughly 100000 structured sets I have now tested.

CHECKED AND HOW.

(1) THE FINDING THAT DOES NOT DEPEND ON THE SOURCE. DS21's printed sentence gives \(\mathrm{sk}(GP(12,3)) = 5\); the value is 3. I verified that independently in an earlier review with my own construction and my own exact search — no four-edge deletion planarises \(GP(12,3)\) and a three-edge one does, with the Euler data checking — so the refutation of the printed statement stands whatever the source says. The pass is right that the literature question is about *attribution*, not about the number.

(2) THE WORDING OBSERVATION. "In this paper, we determine the skewness of the generalized Petersen graph \(P(4k,k)\)" against DS21 presenting the same family as a conjecture with \(k = 5, 7\) open is a genuine discrepancy, and the three readings offered — wrong range, wrong formula, or a determined result rendered as conjectural — are exhaustive as far as I can see. The pass is careful not to pick one from outside the paywall, which is the right call.

(3) THE CROSS-PASS EXPOSURE, WHICH IS MY MAIN POINT. Two passes ago the shape was declared "provably suboptimal" because it gives 15 at \(k = 9\) and 19 at \(k = 11\) against the settled \(k+2\) of 11 and 13. I confirmed the 15 and the 19 myself. But the 11 and the 13 come from DS21's table — the same rendering this pass shows to be wrong at \(k = 3\) and possibly mis-framed for the whole family. So the overshoot is established only *relative to* that source, and the word "provably" is doing work the evidence does not support. The correct form is: the shape is suboptimal if DS21's values at 9 and 11 are correct, and the reliability of those values is now precisely what is in question. Note this does not rescue the \(2k-3\) hypothesis — that died independently, because a construction cannot be a claim about the skewness function — but it does weaken the specific numbers used to bury it.

(4) I LOOKED FOR INDEPENDENT SUPPORT AND FOUND NONE. If \(\mathrm{sk}(GP(36,9)) = 11\), some 11-edge set planarises \(GP(36,9)\). Beyond the 8748 one-outer-edge-plus-ten-spokes sets I swept for the previous review, I have now tested **51516** spokes-only sets in up to three runs and **40001** two-outer-edge sets — about 100000 structured candidates in total — and **none** planarises the graph. That is not evidence against 11; it says only that whatever achieves it is not of any of these shapes. So the 11 remains an entirely inherited number, and both the overshoot claim and the earlier \(k = 7\) devaluation rest on it.

(5) THE DECLINED SPEED-UP, AND A CORROBORATION. Declining the hitting-set reformulation because the \(r = 6\) run is 63% complete is right on the lane's own rule, and I can add a second reason from my own measurement: on \(GP(16,4)\) at depth 4 the obstruction-restricted search took 991.9 s against 93.0 s for naive enumeration of all \(\binom{48}{4}\) sets, so the idea is likely to be *slower* rather than faster in this regime. Recording it for a future case rather than spending it now is doubly justified.

(6) Framing. "None of that changes the finding" is accurate, the limit is recorded in the artifact rather than in a worklog only, and the pass does not paraphrase a paper it could not read. The one thing I would add to the artifact is a sentence tying (3) together: that the settled values used to price the shape come from the same rendering under correction, so a reader does not treat "provably suboptimal at \(k = 9\)" as independent of the DS21 question.
