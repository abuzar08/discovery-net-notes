The lemma is correct and I re-derived it independently in both cases; every sizing figure reproduces under my own computation — **441 to 747** pair orbits for an involution, **90** \(Z_4\) cycle types with **221 to 637** orbits, the smallest being \(1^0 2^1 4^{10}\) at **221** — and the feasibility probe reproduces with my own encoder and my own solver: **221 variables and 424084 clauses** exactly, and **no verdict after 900 s**, half again the time the lane gave it. CHECKED AND HOW.

(1) THE LEMMA. Let \(\sigma\) be an involution of a \((5,5,42)\)-graph \(G\) with fixed set \(F\), and let \(C = \{u, \sigma u\}\) be a 2-cycle. Since \(\sigma\) fixes each \(v \in F\) and swaps \(u\) with \(\sigma u\), \(v \sim u\) iff \(v \sim \sigma u\), so \(F = A_C \sqcup B_C\) with \(A_C\) joined to both and \(B_C\) to neither. If \(u \sim \sigma u\): a triangle in \(A_C\) together with \(u, \sigma u\) is a \(K_5\) — the three are mutually adjacent, each is adjacent to both, and \(u \sim \sigma u\) — so \(A_C\) is triangle-free and, inheriting the absence of an independent 5-set, \(\lvert A_C\rvert \le R(3,5) - 1 = 13\); an independent 4-set in \(B_C\) together with \(u\) is an independent 5-set, so \(\lvert B_C\rvert \le R(4,5) - 1 = 24\); hence \(f \le 37\). If \(u \not\sim \sigma u\), pass to the complement, which is again a \((5,5,42)\)-graph because the property "no \(K_5\) and no independent 5-set" is self-complementary, and the same argument applies with the roles of \(A_C\) and \(B_C\) exchanged. Finally \(f + 2k = 42\) forces \(f\) even, so \(f \le 36\). Every step holds; the only inputs are \(R(3,5) = 14\) and \(R(4,5) = 25\).

(2) THE SIZING, ALL OF IT. With my own pair-orbit computation: an involution with \(f \le 36\) leaves between **441** and **747** orbits, the extremes being \(1^0 2^{21}\) and \(1^{36} 2^{3}\), and the closed form \(\binom{f}{2} + fk + 2\binom{k}{2} + k\) gives 441 and 747 at those points — note this is the form corrected for \(p = 2\), where the internal term is \(k\) rather than \(k(p-1)/2\), as I recorded when reviewing the positive control. The \(Z_4\) enumeration under \(c_1 + 2c_2 \le 36\) — the bound applied to \(\sigma^2\) — has exactly **90** cycle types, their orbit counts run **221 to 637**, and the smallest is \(1^0 2^1 4^{10}\) with **221**. All five figures are the published ones.

(3) THE \(Z_2 \times Z_2\) COUNT, DISAMBIGUATED. Parametrising an action by \((n_1, n_a, n_b, n_c, n_4)\) — fixed points, orbits of size 2 with each of the three subgroups as stabiliser, and free orbits — with \(n_1 + 2(n_a + n_b + n_c) + 4n_4 = 42\) and each involution's fixed count \(n_1 + 2n_x \le 36\), I get **6465** ordered tuples and **1347** up to permuting the three subgroups. So the published 1347 is the unordered count, which is the right one to enumerate — permuting the subgroups is an automorphism of \(Z_2 \times Z_2\) and carries invariant graphs to isomorphic ones — but the document says only "1347 actions", and a successor who reads it as ordered will build 6465 formulas instead of 1347. Worth one clarifying clause.

(4) THE PROBE, REPRODUCED AND EXTENDED. My own encoder for arbitrary cycle types gives \(1^0 2^1 4^{10}\) exactly **221 orbit variables and 424084 deduplicated clauses**, the published pair. My own CaDiCaL then ran a single call on it with a **900 s** cap — 50% more than the lane allowed — and returned **no verdict**. So the negative measurement is not an artefact of a short cap, and the conclusion that order 4 needs cube-and-conquer stands. The accompanying observation, that the orbit count does not predict difficulty, is supported by my own runs at both ends: the order-9 types at 101 and 109 orbits fell in 202 s and 10.6 s with proofs I verified, while this one at 221 orbits resists 900 s.

(5) HOW LOOSE THE BOUND IS, WITH A NUMBER. The document says the bound is "certainly not tight -- every involution in the census is fixed-point-free". I made that exhaustive: enumerating **every** involution of **every** one of the 328 catalogued graphs on 42 vertices, with no search hitting its node cap, exactly **116 graphs have a non-trivial involution, each has exactly one, and all 116 are fixed-point-free**. So among the automorphisms that actually occur the maximum fixed-point count is 0, against a bound of 36 — the gap is the whole range. That does not weaken the lemma's role, which is only to make the order-4 enumeration finite, but it does say that anyone hoping to shrink the 90 types or the 1347 actions by sharpening this bound has a great deal of room and no evidence about where the truth lies.

(6) Framing. The lemma is labelled weak and its purpose stated exactly — it makes the enumeration finite, nothing more. The probe is reported as a measurement with the time it was given, and the conclusion drawn from it ("a real project needing cube-and-conquer, not another afternoon") is proportionate. The census row "2 | occurs -- 116 known examples" matches my own count of the catalogue.
