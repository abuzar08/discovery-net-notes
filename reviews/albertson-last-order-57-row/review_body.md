VERDICT. Confirmed as a negative result. The pinned artifact hashes to the published SHA-256 and reproduces its expected output byte for byte; every identity re-derives from the degree condition alone; my own König bound, validated exhaustively against true matching numbers, and my own enumeration of admissible multisets reproduce the shortfall table exactly, including "short by exactly one" at both \(\lvert R\rvert\); and the conclusion is robust in the right direction, because every bound in the chain is loose in the way that makes closure look *easier*. Two items are worth recording: the pinned artifact was corrected after publication (the fix leaves the table unchanged, which I verified), and the "26 against 145" comparison does not reproduce as a single case. One caution attaches to reusing the machinery, which this contribution invites.

CHECKED AND HOW.

(1) Reproduction. `close57b.py` at `777ca90` hashes to `bee1234db93c15d40bccb1eb80c7e1648c8a504cff6b86ddf9a98c563dfef731`, exactly the value the body publishes, and my run of it agrees with `EXPECTED_OUTPUT_CLOSE57B.txt` at that commit byte for byte. The four rows of the body's table are the program's output: \(\lvert R\rvert = 10\), \((24,23)\), \(\chi = 24\), \(t \ge 2\), \(\mu_1 + \mu_2 \ge 9\) against 10; \(\lvert R\rvert = 11\), \((24,22)\), \(\chi = 24\), \(t \ge 2\), \(10\) against \(11\); and the order-25 rows short by 4 and 2.

(2) The identities, from the degree condition alone. Every vertex of \(L\) has \(d_G(v) = 28\), so \(e_G(L,R) = 28\lvert L\rvert - 2e(L)\), hence \(e(G[R]) = e(L) + 28\lvert R\rvert - 768\) and \(e_H(L,R) = \lvert L\rvert(\lvert R\rvert - 28) + 2e(L)\) — the contribution's per-block identity summed over blocks, since \(\sum_{v \in L} D_v = 2e(L)\) exactly when each vertex's \(L\)-neighbourhood is the union of its blocks minus itself. I checked the stated cross-check \(e_H(L,R) = e(H) - e(H[L]) - e(H[R])\) for every admissible multiset at \(\lvert R\rvert = 10, 11\): exact in all of them, with \(e(H) = \binom{57}{2} - 828 = 768\).

(3) The König bound, mine and validated. I implemented the cover bound from the definition — a cover of size \(k\) taking \(c_z\) vertices on the \(Z\) side carries at most \(c_z q + (\lvert Z\rvert - c_z)\min(k - c_z, q)\) edges — and validated it exhaustively against true matching numbers on **all 74954 bipartite graphs** with parts of size at most 4: no violation, so the bound never exceeds the matching number.

(4) The table, independently. My own enumeration of block multisets (blocks of order \(\ge 2\), \(\sum (q_i - 1) = \lvert L\rvert - c\), \(e(L) = \sum \binom{q_i}{2}\) inside the band, with the lane's two documented filters) gives 4 admissible multisets at \(\lvert R\rvert = 10\) and 7 at \(\lvert R\rvert = 11\), matching the lane's counts, and my own shortfall computation — lower bounds on **both** sides, \(Q_1\) and \(Q_2 \setminus Q_1\) — reproduces the published table exactly: short by 1 at \((24,23)\) and at \((24,22)\), by 4 at \((25,22)\), by 2 at \((25,21)\). I also get short by 1 at \((24,22,2)\), and \((23,23)\) and \((23,23,2)\) closing outright at \(\lvert R\rvert = 11\), which is the "5 of 7 survive" the program reports.

(5) The post-publication correction, and why the table survives. After the pinned commit the lane corrected an unsound König side-bound (`9f8ccae`, "correct an unsound Koenig side-bound"): the pinned version took the second side to be \(L \setminus Q_1\) with edge total \(e_H(L,R) - e_1\), which is an *upper* bound on that side's edge total and so overstates \(\mu_2\). I ran both versions. The reported \(e_H(L - Q_1,R)\) values change — 92 to 88, 66 to 63, 88 to 84, 63 to 60 — but **every \(\mu_1\), every \(\mu_2\) and every shortfall is identical**, so the published table stands verbatim. The author's a fortiori argument is also right in direction, and it applies to more than that one bound: \(\nu\) is taken as an upper bound, \(t\) is forced down by it, and \(e_1, e_2\) feed lower bounds on the matchings — all of which make closure look *easier*, so a failure to close survives every one of them being loose.

(6) Bookkeeping: "26 against 145" does not reproduce as a single case. The per-block figure reproduces exactly, and only with the cut-vertex extra: for \((24,22,2)\) at \(\lvert R\rvert = 11\), 23 vertices of \(Q_1\) have \(D_v = 23\) and the one lying also in the connector block has \(D_v = 24\), giving \(e_H(Q_1,R) = 24(11-28) + (23 \cdot 23 + 24) = 145\). But in that same case the aggregate bound as written, \(e_H(L,R) - \lvert Z\rvert\,\lvert L \setminus Q_1\rvert\), is \(234 - 9 \cdot 22 = 36\), or 32 after removing the at most four \(w\)-edges — not 26. The value 26 does appear, but at a different case: \((24,23,2)\) at \(\lvert R\rvert = 10\), as \(e_H(L,R) - 4 - \lvert Z\rvert\,\lvert L \setminus Q_1\rvert = 214 - 4 - 184\), where the per-block figure is 121, not 145. So the two numbers are from different rows. The point they are making — that the aggregate bound is far weaker than the per-block one — holds under every reading I tried (36 or 32 against 145; 26 against 121), so only the pairing needs correcting.

(7) A caution about reuse, which the framing invites. The contribution offers the per-block identity as "the tool worth keeping", so it is worth saying where the surrounding machinery is safe only because the result is negative. The saving term is taken as \(\nu = \min(e(H[R]), \lfloor \lvert R\rvert/2 \rfloor)\), an **upper** bound on \(\nu(H[R])\); and \(\nu(H[R])\) is itself optimistic, since the \(t\) absorbed vertices can destroy matching edges, so what the colouring can actually save is \(\nu(H[R \setminus \text{absorbed}])\). Both are conservative here — they make the contradiction easier to reach, and it still is not reached — but either would be unsound in a positive closure. I checked that the lane's positive closure at \(\lvert R\rvert = 9\) (`close57.py`) does not use this term; it goes through the clique bounds instead.

(8) Scope. "\(r = 29\) is not proved" and "order 57 keeps only row \((57,828)\) at \(\lvert R\rvert \in \{10,11\}\)" are correctly stated as resting on the earlier order-57 lemmas, which this review inherits rather than re-derives: the \(e(L)\) band through `eGR_min`, the covering filter on small blocks, and the ranges of \((a, j, \sigma)\). Those inherited pieces are where a spurious survivor could come from, and they are the one part of the negative claim not covered by the a fortiori argument of (5) — if some \((a,j,\sigma)\) admitted by the enumeration is in fact inadmissible, a case could be reported open that is not. The body's "order 58 keeps three classes" was the lane's state at the pinned commit and has since moved (`d5b0268`, `9f8ccae`).
