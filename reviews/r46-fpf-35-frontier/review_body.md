CHECKED AND HOW.

(1) The two formulas, from the parameters alone. With my own orbit numbering and my own deduplicated clique and independent-set clauses: \(1^0 5^7\) gives **119 orbit variables and 334369 clauses**, \(1^0 7^5\) gives **85 and 237160** — the published numbers exactly, and the second agrees with what I built independently when reviewing h3295.

(2) The variable decomposition, which is the contribution's actual argument. Classifying each orbit by its representative pair: \(1^0 5^7\) has 105 cross-cycle and 14 internal variables, \(1^0 7^5\) has 70 and 15, matching \(\binom{k}{2}p\) and \(k(p-1)/2\) exactly and summing to the totals. The cross share is 88.2% and 82.4%; the body's "about 85% in both cases" is the average of the two rather than a per-instance figure, which is a rounding of the presentation, not of the argument — the point that the cross block dominates holds in both.

(3) That `symF` is vacuous here is right by construction: its rows are indexed by pairs of fixed vertices, and \(f = 0\) leaves none. My own implementation of it, written for the h3295 composition matrix, likewise imposes nothing below \(f = 2\).

(4) The resistance, on my own formula. I rebuilt both instances with my own encoder plus my own auxiliary-free `symC` — the configuration the contribution used, `symF` being vacuous — and ran a single refutation attempt under the same 1500 s cap: **\(1^0 7^5\): no verdict after 1500 s**; **\(1^0 5^7\): no verdict after 1500 s either**. So "both resist" reproduces, on a different machine and a formula built independently of the lane's code.

(5) The frontier numbers. Enumerating the involution types myself: there are exactly **74** types \(1^f 2^k\) across \(36 \le n \le 39\), and their orbit-variable counts run from **324** (\(1^0 2^{18}\) at \(n = 36\)) to **704** (\(1^{37} 2^1\) at \(n = 39\)) — the published range to the digit. At \(n = 36\) there are 18 types spanning 324 to 596 variables, and my own build of the \(1^0 2^{18}\) formula has 1003833 clauses, against the body's "about \(1.00 \times 10^6\)".

(6) The diagnosis, and what happened to it. The contribution says no lever in the lane acts on the cross-cycle block, names that as the governing parameter, and asks for "a method acting on the cross-block data of a semiregular action with \(f = 0\)". That is precisely what `symS` at h3295 turned out to be — \((k-1)(p-1)\) lex chains on the cross rows, breaking the shift group \(\mathbb{Z}_p^{k-1}\) — and in my review of h3295 I verified with my own code that it is a complete break of that group and that, added to `symC`, it takes \(1^0 7^5\) from the resistance measured here to **UNSAT in 314 s with a drat-trim-verified proof**, on my own formula. Two things are worth recording about that. First, it confirms the diagnosis at the sharpest possible level: the instance that resists here without a cross-block lever falls quickly with one. Second, the lever was **neither of the two candidates named here** — not the full \(S_k\) lex-leader and not the \(\mathbb{Z}_p^{*}\) multiplier action, but the independent per-cycle shifts, a group this contribution does not mention. The identification of *where* the difficulty sits was right; the guesses at *which* group would break it were not, and my review of h3295 also found that one of the two candidates named here, the multiplier action, is the one that fails to compose with `symC`.

(7) Honesty of the framing. "It adds no new refutation", "both resist", "the honest statement is that these two instances are open, not that they are impossible", and the explicit account of two earlier over-strong claims made by measuring one end and generalising — all accurate, and the symmetric measurement of both instances is exactly the discipline those earlier errors called for.
