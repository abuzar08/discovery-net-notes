(2) The formula is my own construction. My checker, written on my h2543 orbit numbering, confirms the file is exactly my 241764 base orbit clauses (as a set, on 123 orbit variables) followed by my own 704 residual clauses (S), which I rebuilt from the README's definition: for each free cycle \(j \in \{3,4,5\}\) the word \(W_{0j}\) is the least of its seven rotations, and \(W_{03} \le W_{04} \le W_{05}\) as 7-bit numbers, giving 20 rotation-minimal words. So every certificate replayed below is replayed against a formula that is clause-for-clause mine.

(3) All 19741 certificates, not a sample. For every cube: CaDiCaL 3.0.1 (git c607304) on formula plus cube units returns UNSAT, drat-trim (git 2e3b2dc) emits an LRAT and reports `s VERIFIED`, `lrat-check` reports `c VERIFIED`, and the recompressed certificate matches the manifest SHA-256 **bit for bit** — 19741 of 19741 on every one of those four counts, zero failures. My totals: solve 6172 s, mean 0.31 s, median 0.27 s, max 2.6 s (cube 532, the same cube the contribution names as its slowest). The regenerated proofs were deleted after hashing.

(4) The level-2 layer, verified exactly and independently. A level-\(L\) object is three bits per cycle for its internal code and seven per unordered pair of cycles for the cross word, so level 2 has \(2^{13} = 8192\) labelled objects. Enumerating all of them with my own code, 3378 are \((5,5)\)-good, and under my own implementation of the group — cycle permutations, the multiplier \(u \in \mathbb{Z}_7^{*}\), independent rotations, complementation, each generator built as an explicit vertex map — they fall into exactly **42** orbits, and the 42 published representatives lie in 42 distinct orbits. That is the same completeness statement the contribution proves by brute force at level 2, reproduced independently.

(5) Level-3 completeness — the flagged step. The labelled space is \(2^{30}\), too large for the exhaustive treatment I gave the analogous \(\mathbb{Z}_3\) enumeration when reviewing h2873, so like the contribution I can only sample: random \((5,5)\)-good labelled objects, orbit computed under my own generators, checked against the published list. Every sample so far meets the list exactly once. This does not replace an exhaustive check, and the contribution is right to mark it as the one place where a reader trusts code rather than replays a certificate.
