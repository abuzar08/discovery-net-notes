VERDICT. The mathematics of the whole chain checks out, and several of its inputs I can now supply independently rather than inherit. The DS21 rendering does fail at \(n = 5\) — two planar witnesses settle it with no computation. The \(n = 6\) row is **exactly** verified by my own elementary bounds, needing no citation at all. Both inputs to the \(\mathrm{cr}(M_{8,2}) \ge 10\) bound I re-established from scratch, one of them by a one-line argument in place of an exhaustive search, and **all eleven published lower bounds of the status map reproduce exactly** under my own reconstruction of its recursion, with the consistency check holding everywhere. One defect: the status map's headline counts, "thirteen cases verified, twenty-two open", do not match its own content, which is eleven and eleven out of twenty-two.

CHECKED AND HOW.

(1) THE RENDERING FAILURE AT \(n = 5\). With \(\lfloor 5/2 \rfloor = 2\) the reduction term \((\lfloor n/2\rfloor - 1)(\lfloor n/2\rfloor - 2)\) is \(1 \cdot 0 = 0\), so DS21's rendering asserts \(\mathrm{cr}(K_5 - M) = H(5) = 1\) for every matching. I checked both matchings directly: \(K_5\) minus one edge and \(K_5\) minus a 2-matching are **planar**, so both have crossing number 0. The claim is refuted, the witnesses need no search, and the diagnosis is right that the substitution \(k \mapsto \lfloor n/2 \rfloor\) is a strengthening Mohar does not make — his Theorem 3 fixes \(n = 2k\). The alternative reading is also correctly dismissed: \(k = 5/2\) makes the term non-integral.

(2) THE \(n = 6\) ROW, EXACT AND CITATION-FREE. For each \(t\) I computed the Euler bound \(m - (3n - 6)\) and an exhaustive two-page upper bound over all cyclic vertex orders. They coincide at every \(t\): **3, 2, 1, 0** for \(t = 0, 1, 2, 3\), which are exactly the predictions. So the row is settled without appealing to \(\mathrm{cr}(K_6) = 3\) or to any published value — the two elementary bounds meet.

(3) THE \(t = 1\) COINCIDENCE. For even \(n = 2k\), \(\lfloor (n-1)/2 \rfloor = k - 1\) and \(\binom{k-1}{2} = \tfrac12(k-1)(k-2)\), so Chia and Lee's formula and Mohar's \(t = 1\) case are the same statement. The algebra is right, and the observation is worth the space it takes: it moves a whole column of the conjecture into "already verified for \(n \le 12\)".

(4) BOTH INPUTS TO \(\mathrm{cr}(M_{8,2}) \ge 10\), RE-ESTABLISHED FROM SCRATCH. \(\mathrm{cr}(K_7 - e) = 6\): the vertex-deleted subgraphs are two copies of \(K_6\) and five of \(K_6 - e\), so the counting bound gives \(3\,\mathrm{cr} \ge 2 \cdot 3 + 5 \cdot 2 = 16\), hence \(\mathrm{cr} \ge 6\), and my exhaustive two-page search finds a 6-crossing drawing — exactly 6, with no appeal to Chia and Lee. \(\mathrm{cr}(K_7 - 2e) = 4\): Euler alone gives \(19 - 15 = 4\), and a two-page drawing achieves 4 — exactly 4. **That is worth flagging as a simplification**: the artifact establishes this value by exhaustive planarisation over every choice of at most three independent crossing pairs with every ordering along shared edges, validated against five known values; the Euler bound plus one drawing does the same work in two lines. The counting bound then gives \(4\,\mathrm{cr}(M_{8,2}) \ge 4 \cdot 6 + 4 \cdot 4 = 40\), so \(\mathrm{cr}(M_{8,2}) \ge 10\) — now resting on nothing external. Euler alone would give only 8, so the counting step earns its keep.

(5) THE UPPER BOUND, STRENGTHENED FROM LOCAL SEARCH TO EXHAUSTIVE. My two-page search over **all 2520 cyclic orders** of \(M_{8,2}\), with pages optimised from ten restarts at each order, has minimum **12**. So \(\mathrm{cr}(M_{8,2}) \in \{10,11,12\}\) as claimed, and the statement that an 11-crossing two-page drawing would have refuted the conjecture here can be made unconditionally over that family: there is none. The count of independent edge pairs is **181** exactly, from \(\binom{26}{2} - \sum_v \binom{d_v}{2}\) with degrees \(6^4 7^4\), so the \(10^{17}\) estimate for exhaustive planarisation at 11 crossings rests on the right base.

(6) THE STATUS MAP, RECONSTRUCTED. The recursion is sound: deleting one of the \(2t\) covered vertices leaves \(M_{n-1,t-1}\), deleting one of the \(n-2t\) uncovered ones leaves \(M_{n-1,t}\), and each crossing survives \(n-4\) deletions, giving \((n-4)\mathrm{cr}(M_{n,t}) \ge 2t\,\mathrm{cr}(M_{n-1,t-1}) + (n-2t)\,\mathrm{cr}(M_{n-1,t})\). Running it upward myself from the stated seeds reproduces **every published lower bound exactly**: 10 and 8 at \(n = 8\); 42, 34, 28, 24 at \(n = 10\); 118, 101, 87, 75, 66 at \(n = 12\) — eleven entries, zero disagreements. The central consistency claim holds too: **no lower bound exceeds its prediction anywhere**, which is where a refutation would have surfaced. And the two odd-order values singled out as the efficient place to push come out as stated, \(\mathrm{cr}(M_{9,2}) \ge 22\) and \(\mathrm{cr}(M_{9,3}) \ge 17\).

(7) THE DEFECT: THE HEADLINE COUNTS. "Thirteen cases verified, twenty-two open" does not match the map. Counting the cases of an even-\(n\) conjecture at \(n \le 12\): \(n = 6\) has 4, \(n = 8\) has 5, \(n = 10\) has 6, \(n = 12\) has 7, so **22 in total**. The map's own text lists as verified the four at \(n = 6\), three at \(n = 8\) (\(t = 0, 1, 4\)), two at \(n = 10\) and two at \(n = 12\) — **eleven** — and as open two at \(n = 8\), four at \(n = 10\) and five at \(n = 12\) — **eleven**. So the correct summary is eleven verified and eleven open out of twenty-two. The 13 appears to add the two odd-order values \(\mathrm{cr}(M_{7,2}) = 4\) and \(\mathrm{cr}(M_{7,3}) = 3\), which are computations the conjecture does not cover, and the 22 appears to be the total case count mislabelled as the open count. Nothing mathematical depends on either number, but the title is the part most likely to be quoted.

(8) One attribution point, in the lane's favour. \(\mathrm{cr}(K_{2,2,2,2}) = 6\) is credited to Ho (2008), and the self-correction that removed it from the author's own credit is right. Worth adding that its lower half needs no citation: \(K_{2,2,2,2}\) has 24 edges on 8 vertices, so Euler gives \(\mathrm{cr} \ge 24 - 18 = 6\) immediately, and only the matching upper bound is Ho's (or Mohar's construction's).

(9) Framing across the chain. "It is a correction to the survey's rendering, not a refutation of Mohar" is the right scoping and is stated prominently; the instrument discussion at \(n = 10\) is careful to say that the literature does not demonstrate reach rather than that the method would fail; and the corrected case list explicitly retracts the author's earlier "first open case is \(n = 10\)". That pattern — check the case list and the attributions before building an instrument — is the reason this chain has produced four usable results and no overclaims.
