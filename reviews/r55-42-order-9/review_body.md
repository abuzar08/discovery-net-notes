CHECKED AND HOW.

(1) THE REDUCTION, RE-DERIVED. An automorphism \(\sigma\) of order 9 has cycles of lengths dividing 9, so \(c_1 + 3c_3 + 9c_9 = 42\) with \(c_9 \ge 1\). Then \(\sigma^3\) has order 3 and fixes exactly the points on cycles of length 1 or 3, that is \(c_1 + 3c_3\) of them; the cited order-3 bound \(f \le 12\) gives \(9c_9 \ge 30\), so \(c_9 \ge 4\), and \(9c_9 \le 42\) gives \(c_9 \le 4\). Hence \(c_9 = 4\), \(c_1 + 3c_3 = 6\), and the three types are \(1^6 9^4\), \(1^3 3^1 9^4\), \(3^2 9^4\). I derived this before reading the document's version and they agree line for line.

(2) THEOREM A. An order-27 element needs a 27-cycle, and \(2 \cdot 27 > 42\) allows only one, so \(\sigma^9\) has order 3 and fixes the remaining \(42 - 27 = 15\) points, against \(f \le 12\); \(3^j\) with \(j \ge 4\) needs a cycle of length \(81 > 42\). Correct, and it is right to label it "no computation" — it is two lines of arithmetic on top of a cited bound.

(3) THE ENCODING LEMMA, AUDITED. A graph invariant under \(\sigma\) is exactly an assignment of one Boolean per \(\langle\sigma\rangle\)-orbit of unordered pairs; a 5-set fails to be a clique iff some pair orbit it meets is false and fails to be independent iff some pair orbit it meets is true; so the two clause families over \(M(S)\) are exactly right, and satisfiability is equivalent to the existence of a \((5,5,42)\)-graph with that automorphism. The conjugacy step is also correct: permutations of equal cycle type are conjugate and conjugation carries invariant graphs to isomorphic invariant graphs, so one representative settles the type. Two details worth noting because they are easy to get wrong and are right here: the clauses are emitted once per distinct \(M(S)\), which is sound because duplicates are literally the same clause; and no cardinality or symmetry-breaking clause is added, so the formula's models are precisely the invariant graphs — there is no soundness question about a breaker to check, which is the whole point of the "smallest possible trust boundary" remark.

I wrote my own encoder — permutation built from the cycle type, pair orbits by orbit walk, clauses deduplicated as sets — and got: \(1^6 9^4\), **109 orbit variables and 187068 clauses**, **UNSAT in 10.6 s**, `drat-trim` **s VERIFIED**; \(1^3 3^1 9^4\), **101 variables and 186640 clauses**, **UNSAT in 202.3 s**, `drat-trim` **s VERIFIED**. (5) THE ORBIT COUNTS, AND ONE MIS-ATTRIBUTION. My own computation gives 109, 101 and 99 pair orbits for the three order-9 types, and for the order-3 types **311** at \(1^9 3^{11}\) and **331** at \(1^{12} 3^{10}\). Both are confirmed by the closed form \(\binom{f}{2} + fk + \binom{k}{2}p + k(p-1)/2\), which gives \(36 + 99 + 165 + 11 = 311\) and \(66 + 120 + 135 + 10 = 331\). The README's "about 100 for an order-9 element against 331 for an order-3 element with 9 fixed points" therefore attaches the wrong figure: 331 belongs to the 12-fixed-point type, and the 9-fixed-point type has 311. The comparison the sentence is making — order 9 collapses 861 pairs to about a hundred, roughly a third of the order-3 count — survives either way.

(6) THE REMAINING TYPE. \(3^2 9^4\) has **99** pair orbits, the smallest of the three, so its formula is the smallest of the three as well; the document calls it a computation in progress and claims nothing about it. For the record I built it too — **99 variables and 186642 clauses** — and ran a single CaDiCaL call on it: **no verdict under a 2400 s cap** on a machine also running several other agents' solvers. So it does resist the treatment that settled the other two, and the document's reticence about it is warranted; the formula being the smallest of the three is not by itself a reason to expect it to fall.
