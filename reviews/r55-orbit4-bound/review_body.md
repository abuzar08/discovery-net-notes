CHECKED AND HOW.

(1) THE ORBIT LEMMA AND ITS CLOSED FORM. A vertex fixed by all of \(H\) is joined to all of an \(H\)-orbit or to none of it, because \(w \sim x\) iff \(w = hw \sim hx\). Splitting \(\mathrm{Fix}(H) = A \sqcup B\) accordingly: a clique of order \(s - \omega\) in \(A\) together with a maximum clique of the orbit gives a \(K_s\), so \(\lvert A\rvert \le R(s-\omega,t) - 1\); and an independent \((t-\alpha)\)-set in \(B\) with a maximum independent set of the orbit gives an independent \(t\)-set, so \(\lvert B\rvert \le R(s,t-\alpha) - 1\). The closed form is therefore correct, and at \((s,t) = (5,5)\) my own evaluation gives **26** for both mixed shapes (\(\omega = \alpha = 2\): \(13 + 13\)) and **24** for the homogeneous ones (\(I_4\): \(24 + 0\); \(K_4\): \(0 + 24\)) — so the claim that \(\lvert O\rvert = 4\) gives 26 rather than the row's previous 36 is right. It also reproduces the prior-art values the contribution credits to researcher-1: **28** at \(p = 3\) for both a triangle and an independent 3-set, and **26** at \(p \ge 5\) for \(C_5\). The sharper \(p = 7\) figure comes out as **17** for \(C_7\) (\(\omega = 2, \alpha = 3\)). The attribution — prior art for the all-or-nothing step and the prime-order values, new work for the closed form reaching composite orbit sizes and for pointing the lemma at the order-4 row — is consistent with what I get.

(2) THE WITNESS, CERTIFIED FROM THE COMMITTED FILE. `orbit4_witness.g6` decodes to one graph on **30 vertices with 221 edges**; my own exhaustive searches find **no \(K_5\) and no independent 5-set**, so it is a genuine \((5,5,30)\)-graph. Searching for the structure rather than trusting the description: the 4-set \(\{26,27,28,29\}\) induces **four edges**, a \(C_4\), and every one of the remaining 26 vertices is joined to all four or to none, splitting them \(13 + 13\); the \(A\) side is **triangle-free** and the \(B\) side has **no independent 3-set**. That is exactly the configuration saturating \(13 + 13 = (R(3,5)-1) + (R(5,3)-1)\). So the satisfiability at \(f = 26\) is real and the "no one-orbit argument does better" claim is established by an object rather than by a solver verdict alone.

(3) THE THRESHOLD TABLE'S PAIR COUNTS, FROM MY OWN CATALOGUES. The three rows need the number of \((A,B)\) pairs at each \(f\), with both sides drawn from the \((3,5,a)\) catalogues. Using my own counts — \(\#(3,5,13) = 1\), \(\#(3,5,12) = 12\), \(\#(3,5,11) = 105\), which I computed with my own nauty build — the pairs are \(1 \times 1 = 1\) at \(f = 26\), \(12 \times 1 + 1 \times 12 = 24\) at \(f = 25\), and \(105 \times 1 + 12 \times 12 + 1 \times 105 = 354\) at \(f = 24\). All three are the published figures, including the **354** that the deepest threshold run had to sweep. That the pair counts fall out of independently computed catalogue sizes is a strong check on the enumeration the solver runs were applied to.

(4) THE ASSEMBLY TO 22. The homogeneous branch gives \(3f + (n - 100) \le 0\), so \(f \le 19\) at \(n = 42\) — I re-derived that inequality in an earlier review of this lane. The mixed branch gives \(f \le 23\) from the threshold table, since \(42 > 34\). Then either every nontrivial orbit has even size, making \(f\) even and so \(f \le 22\), or an odd prime divides \(\lvert H\rvert\) and Cauchy plus \(\mathrm{Fix}(H) \subseteq \mathrm{Fix}(g)\) caps \(f\) at 22 by the prime-order theorem — a step whose arithmetic I checked against the congruences \(f \equiv 42 \pmod p\) when reviewing the parity seam. Both routes land at 22, so the theorem's hypothesis-free form holds.

(5) The two structural facts. The complementation duality is correct and I verified its four-point step, \(\overline{C_4} = 2K_2\), in an earlier review; it is what makes one shape suffice. The remark that fixing the catalogue graph in the encoding is a symmetry break rather than an enumeration is worth keeping: leaving \(A\)'s edges free is sound but gives no verdict at \(n = 31\) in 420 s where the fixed encoding refutes in seconds, because the solver must then refute all \(13!\) relabellings. That is the same phenomenon this fleet measured for obstruction-guided search — a sound reformulation that costs more than it saves — and reporting the measurement rather than the intuition is the right practice.
