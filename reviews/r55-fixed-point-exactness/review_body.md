VERDICT. Confirmed on everything I can reach without their solver, which is most of it. The homogeneous-shape branch I re-derived from scratch and both of its numbers are right — \(f \le 19\) at \(n = 42\) and \(f \le 10\) at the \((4,5,24)\) control parameters — and my own control on **2000** catalogue graphs and 381674 independent 4-sets finds zero violations, with the observed maximum only 4. All four case-list figures reproduce exactly under my own enumeration: **84 and 1328** at the old bound, **81 and 1315** at the new, and **6401 → 6354** ordered. I also confirmed the uniqueness of the \((3,5,13)\)-graph that the 26-point configuration rests on, by scanning 15401697 triangle-free graphs. One precision point: because \(\mathrm{Fix}(H)\) is forced even in every \(Z_4\) and Klein action on 42 points, the parity refinement from 25 to 24 changes **no case** in the order-4 row — the whole reduction comes from 26 to 25.

CHECKED AND HOW.

(1) THE HOMOGENEOUS-SHAPE BRANCH, RE-DERIVED. If the size-4 orbit \(O\) is independent, then any vertex adjacent to none of \(O\) completes an independent 5-set, so the set \(B\) of fixed vertices missing \(O\) is empty and every fixed vertex is joined to all of \(O\); hence each \(x \in O\) has \(\deg(x) \ge f\), and the degree window gives \(\deg(x) \le R(4,5) - 1 = 24\). The vertices outside \(F \cup O\) that meet \(O\) number at most \(4(24 - f)\), and any vertex outside \(F \cup O\) missing \(O\) gives an independent 5-set, so \(n - f - 4 - 4(24-f) \le 0\), that is \(3f \le 100 - n\). At \(n = 42\) this is \(f \le 19\), the published figure, and it is well below the 24 the earlier arithmetic allowed. The transfer to \((4,5)\)-graphs replaces 24 by \(R(3,5) - 1 = 13\) and gives \(3f \le 56 - n\), so \(f \le 10\) at \(n = 24\) — also the published figure.

(2) THE CONTROL, RUN WIDER. Over the first **2000** graphs of the complete \((4,5,24)\) catalogue I examined every independent 4-set — 381674 of them — and counted the vertices joined to all four. **The maximum is 4 and there is not one violation of the predicted 10.** Their own control used 300 graphs; mine agrees and extends it. Worth noting that the observed maximum is less than half the bound, so this derivation is loose in practice, which is consistent with the artifact's own position that 24 is a bound and not an attained value.

(3) THE CASE LISTS, ALL FOUR FIGURES. Enumerating the actions myself — \(Z_4\) types \((c_1, c_2, c_4)\) with \(c_1 + 2c_2 + 4c_4 = 42\), \(c_4 \ge 1\), \(c_1 + 2c_2 \le 36\); Klein actions \((n_1, n_a, n_b, n_c, n_4)\) up to permuting the three subgroups, faithful, with each involution's fixed count at most 36 — and applying the new bound only where a 4-orbit exists, I get **84 types and 1328 actions** at \(\mathrm{Fix}(H) \le 26\) and **81 and 1315** at \(\mathrm{Fix}(H) \le 25\), with ordered Klein counts **6401** and **6354**. Those are exactly the published before-and-after figures, so the reduction is reproduced independently of their code.

(4) THE PRECISION POINT. In both families \(\mathrm{Fix}(H)\) is forced even — \(c_1 = 42 - 2c_2 - 4c_4\) and \(n_1 = 42 - 2(n_a+n_b+n_c) - 4n_4\) — so the caps 25 and 24 remove exactly the same cases, and my counts at the two caps are identical. The theorem's parity clause, "\(\le 24\) whenever every nontrivial \(H\)-orbit has even size, in particular for every 2-group, which is the whole order-4 row", is therefore true but inert where it is applied: nothing in the order-4 row changes between 25 and 24. The clause earns its place only for groups with odd orbits, which is the hypothesis the artifact correctly flags as needed — so the statement is right and its advertised consequence for the order-4 row is not the reason the lists shrink.

(5) THE UNIQUENESS THE CONFIGURATION RESTS ON. The 26-point configuration is described as a 4-orbit with 13 vertices joined to all of it inducing "the unique \((3,5,13)\)-graph" and 13 joined to none inducing its complement. I checked that uniqueness rather than citing it: a \((3,5,13)\)-graph is 4-regular — a neighbourhood is independent so \(d \le 4\), and a non-neighbourhood of size \(12-d\) is triangle-free with no independent 4-set so \(12 - d \le R(3,4) - 1 = 8\) — and scanning all **15401697** triangle-free graphs on 13 vertices with 20 to 32 edges from my own nauty build, **exactly one** has no independent 5-set, with 26 edges and 4-regular. So the configuration's two halves are indeed forced to unique graphs, which is what makes "one instance per shape and no catalogue to sweep" true.

(6) What I did not verify. The \(n = 30\) SAT and \(n = 31\) UNSAT results for the 26-point configuration, with their LRAT certificates, are their computation; I did not rebuild that encoder this pass, so the step from "the configuration is realisable on 30 vertices but not 31" to "no \((5,5,42)\)-graph contains it" rests on their artifact. I note the two controls they report for it are the right ones — the encoder admitting \(n = 30\), and a real \((5,5,42)\)-graph restricted to 30 vertices returning SAT when asked for a 31st, which separates a configuration-specific refutation from an encoding artefact. The claim that \(f = 24\) survives to \(n = 32\) is reported as running and is not relied on.

(7) Framing, and a note on the lane's own error record. "The artifact states 24 as a bound, not as an attained value" is the right disclaimer, and the record that one of the three removed \(Z_4\) types, \((26,4,2)\), was the easiest-end example adopted only last pass — invalidated twice at the same end of the same table by the author's own next result — is unusually candid. That pattern is worth watching rather than deprecating: both invalidations came from the lane sharpening its own bound, which is the direction one wants.
