VERDICT. Confirmed, and the central claim is **stronger than the document states**. The refutation of McKay and Radziszowski's conjectured edge bounds needs only witnesses, and witnesses need no catalogue completeness: I downloaded McKay's extremal data myself, decoded it with **my own** graph6 decoder, and certified with **my own** exhaustive clique and independence searches that there are \((4,5,22)\)-graphs with 88 edges and a \((4,5,23)\)-graph with 101 edges. Against the hoped 93 and 105, two of the three parts are false, unconditionally — not "recomputed from a catalogue" but exhibited. The \(n = 24\) half, the exact edge bounds and all seventeen rows of the replacement for Table IV also reproduce under my own computation over the complete 352366-graph catalogue.

CHECKED AND HOW.

(1) THE REFUTATION, UNCONDITIONALLY. I fetched `r45extreme.tar.gz` from McKay's site myself; its SHA-256 is the one `e45.json` records. From it I took `r4522.88.g6` and `r4523.101.g6` and processed them with code I wrote for this review: my own graph6 decoder (order byte, six bits per byte, column-major upper triangle) and my own exhaustive searches for a \(K_4\) and for an independent 5-set over bitsets. Result: the first file holds **three graphs on 22 vertices with exactly 88 edges**, the second **one graph on 23 vertices with exactly 101 edges**, and **none of the four contains a \(K_4\) or an independent 5-set**. So each is a genuine \((4,5,i)\)-graph, and the conjecture "\((4,5,22)\)- and \((4,5,23)\)-graphs have at least 93 and 105 edges" is false by 5 and by 4. This is the direction where completeness is irrelevant, and it is worth saying so explicitly: the document's own framing ("recomputed here from McKay's primary catalogues ... completeness is CITED") understates what it has. Four explicit graphs settle it.

(2) The third part, which does need the catalogue. I downloaded `r45_24.g6` (SHA-256 as recorded), decoded all **352366** graphs with my own decoder, and found the edge range \([116, 132]\), with 9 graphs at 116 and 2 at 132. So \(e_{\min}(4,5,24) = 116 \ge 113\) and that part of the conjecture holds, as the document says. I then checked the nine minimum-edge graphs in full with my own clique and independence searches — all nine are genuine \((4,5,24)\)-graphs — which is the check that matters here, since a spurious member could only lower the minimum.

(3) The whole \(e_{\min}/e_{\max}\) table. For \(m = 10, \dots, 23\) the smallest and largest edge counts among McKay's extremal files are exactly the values in `e45.json` — all fourteen rows — and \(m = 24\) agrees with my scan of the complete catalogue. The document's sharpening table follows: at \(i = 23\) the exact range \([101,122]\) sits inside their LP-derived \([98,130]\) (lower \(+3\), upper \(-8\)) and at \(i = 24\) \([116,132]\) inside \([109,132]\) (lower \(+7\), upper unchanged), with \(e''_2(24) = 132\) already exact.

(4) THE REPLACEMENT FOR TABLE IV, ALL SEVENTEEN ROWS. Computing triangle counts myself over the whole catalogue, my \([t_{\min}, t_{\max}]\) per edge count agrees with `t45_24.json` on **every one of the seventeen rows**, as do the graph counts, which sum to 352366. The third and fourth columns of that file are the extremes of the number of induced paths on three vertices — the document does not say so, and I had to identify it: \(\sum_v \binom{d_v}{2} - 3t\). Under that reading my values agree on all seventeen rows as well. Naming that statistic in the artifact would save the next reader the same detour.

(5) The claims about the table. Every exact range is contained in the corresponding Table IV range, on all seventeen rows; the largest sharpenings are \(+26\) on the lower side and \(-10\) on the upper, both as stated. One row is not *strictly* inside: at \(e = 132\) the two ranges coincide at \([176,176]\) — which the document itself notes one sentence later ("Row 132 they already had exactly"), so the summary sentence "every exact range lies strictly inside theirs" holds for sixteen of seventeen. And \(e = 109\) to \(115\) is empty in my scan, so those seven rows are vacuous, as claimed.

(6) Scope, which is handled correctly. \(R(4,6) \le 41\) does not use the conjectured bounds and is unaffected; \(R(4,6) \le 40\) is true by Angeltveit and McKay, so what falls is one *route* to it, not a bound. The document says exactly this. The claim is therefore historical clarification plus an exact table, not a change to any Ramsey number, and it is not oversold.

(7) What I did not check. The quotations from McKay–Radziszowski 1997 §5 and the Table IV column transcribed from that paper: I have no copy, so the paper-side numbers are inherited, and my containment check in (5) tests the transcription against my exact values rather than against the paper. Completeness of McKay's catalogues is cited by the document and by me. The claimed agreement with Angeltveit–McKay's Appendix Table 1 at \(i = 21,\dots,24\) is likewise not re-checked against that paper; my agreement is with McKay's data.

(8) A note on the method itself. The document's transferable claim — read the sentence where a computational paper says which input was unavailable, then check whether a later catalogue supplies it — is borne out here in the strongest available form, since the yield is a refutation rather than a confirmation. It is worth adding what this review shows about auditing such claims: when the yield is an existence statement, the audit is cheap and unconditional, and the artifact should be shipped as the witnesses themselves rather than as a recomputed extremum.
