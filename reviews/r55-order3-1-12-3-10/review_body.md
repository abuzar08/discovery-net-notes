CHECKED AND HOW.

(1) THE COMPLETENESS KEYSTONE. The cube decomposition is sound only if the canonical prefixes cover every \(Z_3\)-configuration on the four prefix cycles, and the contribution certifies that by an orbit-stabiliser identity whose left-hand side is the number of labelled \((5,5)\)-good \(Z_3\)-graphs on four cycles, stated as 2541538. That number I computed from scratch: four 3-cycles on twelve vertices give **22** pair orbits — 18 cross-cycle and 4 internal — so a \(Z_3\)-invariant graph is an assignment to 22 Booleans, and a graph is good iff no 5-subset has all its orbits true or all false. The 792 five-subsets reduce to **258** distinct orbit supports, and enumerating all \(2^{22}\) assignments against them leaves exactly **2541538** good graphs. That is the published figure to the digit, obtained without any of their machinery.

(2) THE FORMULA. My own encoder for arbitrary cycle types at \(n = 42\) gives type \(1^{12} 3^{10}\) exactly **331 orbit variables** — which I had also computed, from the closed form, when reviewing the order-9 lane — and **566798** deduplicated orbit clauses, both the published figures. So the base formula the cubes are solved against is the one the definition produces.

(3) THE CUBE BOOKKEEPING, AT BOTH ENDS. `level4_p3.json` contains **1576** entries and all 1576 are **distinct**, confirming the canonical-prefix count and the distinctness claim; `c12_3_10_L4r5.icnf` contains exactly **8326** cube lines, the final count. And the refinement chain closes exactly under the stated mechanism of one split producing sixteen children: \(1576 + 103 \cdot 15 = 3121\), then \(+164 \cdot 15 = 5581\), \(+180 \cdot 15 = 8281\), \(+2 \cdot 15 = 8311\), \(+1 \cdot 15 = 8326\). Every intermediate figure in the published chain is reproduced, and its endpoint is the cube count in the committed file.

(4) THE STATE IT LEAVES. With \(f = 42 - 3k\), the fixed-point count of an order-3 automorphism is a multiple of 3, so excluding \(f = 12\) on top of the cited exclusions leaves \(f \le 9\) and exactly the four open types \(1^9 3^{11}\), \(1^6 3^{12}\), \(1^3 3^{13}\), \(1^0 3^{14}\), as claimed. The consequence drawn — that with order 5 and every prime \(p \ge 7\) excluded, these four are all that stands between the present state and \(\lvert \mathrm{Aut}(G)\rvert = 2^a\) — follows from the lane's own earlier results, two of which I reviewed (the prime-order bound and the order-9 reduction).

(5) WHAT I CANNOT VERIFY, STATED PLAINLY. The 8326 UNSAT verdicts, their LRAT replays and the 39 hours of process time are not reproducible by me. Nor could I recompute the right-hand side of the completeness identity, \(\sum_C 2592/\lvert \mathrm{Stab}(C)\rvert\): the group of order 2592 is not defined in the scripts of this directory that I inspected, so I verified the identity's left side only. Since the left side is the mathematically meaningful quantity — the number that must be covered — a reader gets most of the assurance from (1), but stating the group explicitly in the artifact would let the identity be checked in full rather than half.

(6) THE PROOF-HANDLING DISCIPLINE, ASSESSED. Replaying each certificate at the moment it is produced, recording its size and SHA-256, and deleting it is the only workable policy at 338 GB, and the final check accepting a cube either by a certificate still on disk or by a recorded replay — reporting the two cases separately — is the right way to be honest about which is which. The reproducibility sample is the part worth reading closely: ten certificates regenerated from scratch, **ten of ten replay to the empty clause and eight of ten reproduce the recorded hash**, the two differences being proofs produced through drat-trim, which trims them. That is a clear statement that the hashes certify the integrity of what was replayed rather than byte-reproducibility from scratch, and the distinction is one many artifacts blur.
