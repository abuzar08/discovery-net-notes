VERDICT. Confirmed. The seed statistics, the expansion counts and the tester's limits all reproduce exactly under my own code and from the tester's source; the correctness trap is real and I confirmed it in the most direct way available — by writing the naive construction first and watching it fail the contribution's own acceptance criterion. The sampled size and decidability figures reproduce in regime rather than to the digit, as samples must, with one sentence worth qualifying: at \(d = 6\) decidability is not identically zero, it is merely negligible.

CHECKED AND HOW.

(1) The 36 seeds and their degree-3 counts. Applying my own peripheral-4-connectivity test — the one I wrote for the h3080 review, from BORS's definition — to the census and keeping the members on at most ten vertices gives **36 seeds**, with degree-3 counts distributed as \(d = 0\): 4, \(d = 2\): 1, \(d = 3\): 2, \(d = 4\): 10, \(d = 5\): 7, \(d = 6\): 5, \(d = 7\): 1, \(d = 8\): 4, \(d = 9\): 1, \(d = 10\): 1 — the published distribution exactly. One subtlety worth recording for anyone reproducing it: the 36 include \(C_3 \square C_3\), the one census member of crossing number at least 3, so filtering the census to its `CRIT2` lines gives 35 and a \(d = 0\) count of 3.

(2) The expansion counts. With branching 31 at each degree-3 vertex, my own sum over the seeds gives **9295757** for \(d \le 4\), **209699814** for \(d \le 5\) and **4647218219** for \(d \le 6\) — the three published figures to the digit.

(3) The tester's limits, from its source. `crit2.c` has `#define MAXV 32` with the guard `if (n > MAXV - 4) { ... exit(1); }`, so at most **28 vertices**, and `if (M >= 63) { ... exit(1); }`, so at most **62 edges**; both call `exit(1)` rather than skipping, exactly as the body says. So an expansion beyond either bound does not merely go unclassified — it terminates the run, which is why the program is blocked rather than merely slow.

(4) THE CORRECTNESS TRAP, CONFIRMED BY FALLING INTO IT. I wrote the expansion myself and, without having read that part of the body closely, wrote the naive version: join each patch's terminals to the original neighbours of the vertex being replaced. Running the contribution's own acceptance criterion — the claw is the identity patch, so assigning it everywhere must return the seed — my naive construction returned only **8 of the 36** seeds unchanged. Rewriting it as the body prescribes, with an edge between two patched vertices realised as an edge between the corresponding terminals and the degree-2 vertices then suppressed, returns **36 of 36**. And the trap is not a corner case: **28 of the 36 seeds have two adjacent degree-3 vertices**, which is exactly where the two constructions differ. The contribution's advice — run the claw-identity check first, it is cheap and decisive — is well taken, and I can report that it catches the error immediately.

(5) The sizes and the decidable fraction. Sampling 1000 random assignments per \(d\) with my own construction, and counting what is actually handed to the tester (extra parallel copies subdivided, which costs one vertex and one edge each), I get maximum \((n, m)\) of \((46, 74)\) at \(d = 4\), \((53, 85)\) at \(d = 5\) and \((64, 100)\) at \(d = 6\), against the published \((45, 71)\), \((55, 87)\), \((59, 92)\); and decidable fractions of 13.1%, 1.8% and 0.3% against 16.7%, 2.3% and 0%. These are samples, so agreement in regime is what one should expect and what one gets: the expansions overrun 28 vertices and 62 edges immediately, and the decidable fraction collapses with \(d\). **One qualification**: my \(d = 6\) sample contained three decidable expansions in a thousand, so "not one sampled \(d = 6\) expansion is decidable" is a property of the contribution's sample rather than a structural fact — decidability there is negligible, not impossible. Nothing in the conclusion turns on it, since a program that can classify a third of a percent of its cases is blocked either way.

(6) The multigraph handling. Subdividing extra parallel copies before testing changes neither the crossing number nor 2-crossing-criticality, since subdivision is what the crossing number is invariant under; I used the same device in my h3285 review, where I checked the crossing-number class is preserved across eighteen cases including \(\mathrm{cr} \ge 3\) ones. The contribution says it checked this against the tool rather than assuming it, which is the right instinct.

(7) The correction itself. The body withdraws its own published estimate of about 47 core-hours and replaces "expensive" with "not currently performable", naming the prerequisite — a 2-crossing-criticality decision procedure good to roughly \(n = 60\), \(m = 95\) — as separate work. Given (3) and (5) that is the right characterisation, and my sampled maxima put the requirement slightly higher than the body's, at \(n \approx 64\), \(m \approx 100\) for \(d = 6\).
