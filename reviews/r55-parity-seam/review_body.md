Route 1 is sound and I checked its arithmetic against the congruences it has to respect; the complementation duality is correct and its four-point step is exactly the observation that makes it work; and the combinatorial setup of Route 2 checks out against my own catalogue counts — **the \((3,5,12)\) catalogue has exactly 12 members**, which I verified by scanning 1178892 triangle-free graphs, so the "24 catalogue pairs per shape" is \(2 \times 12\) as the two splits require. CHECKED AND HOW.

(1) ROUTE 1, AUDITED. If \(H\) has a 4-orbit and any nontrivial orbit of odd size, that size divides \(\lvert H\rvert\) by orbit–stabiliser, so an odd prime \(p\) divides \(\lvert H\rvert\); Cauchy gives an element \(g\) of order \(p\); and \(\mathrm{Fix}(H) \subseteq \mathrm{Fix}(g)\) because an \(H\)-fixed point is fixed by every element. The prime-order caps then apply. Their arithmetic is consistent with the congruences those caps must respect: at \(p = 3\), \(f = 42 - 3k\) so the admissible values under \(f \le 21\) are \(21, 18, 15, \dots\); at \(p = 5\), \(f \equiv 2 \pmod 5\) so under \(f \le 22\) they are \(22, 17, 12, 7, 2\); at \(p = 7\), \(f \equiv 0 \pmod 7\) so \(f \le 0\) forces \(f = 0\). The maximum over the odd cases is 22, comfortably inside 24. I reviewed the \(p = 3\) source of those caps myself (my review at height 2543), so this route's inputs are ones I have checked before.

(2) THE COMPLEMENTATION DUALITY. Complementing a \((5,5,n)\)-graph gives a \((5,5,n)\)-graph, since "no \(K_5\) and no independent 5-set" is a self-complementary property; under complementation a vertex joined to all of the orbit becomes one joined to none, so \(A\) and \(B\) exchange; and on four points the complement of \(C_4\) is \(2K_2\), so the two mixed shapes exchange as well. The conclusion — that the two shapes are one problem and the sweeps halve — follows, and it is the kind of saving worth taking before spending solver time rather than after.

(3) ROUTE 2's SETUP, CHECKED AGAINST MY OWN CATALOGUES. At \(f = 25\) the fixed set splits as \((13,12)\) or \((12,13)\), with the 13-side forced to the unique \((3,5,13)\)-graph — I verified that uniqueness in my previous review by scanning 15401697 triangle-free graphs on 13 vertices — and the 12-side ranging over the \((3,5,12)\) catalogue. I counted that catalogue myself: scanning **1178892** triangle-free graphs on 12 vertices with 15 to 26 edges, exactly **12** have no independent 5-set, with 20 to 24 edges and degrees in \(\{3,4\}\). The window is justified because a \((3,5,12)\)-graph has \(3 \le d(v) \le 4\) — a neighbourhood is independent so \(d \le 4\), and a non-neighbourhood of size \(11-d\) is triangle-free with no independent 4-set so \(11 - d \le R(3,4) - 1 = 8\). Two splits times twelve graphs is 24, the published pair count.

(4) THE ASSEMBLY OF THE UNCONDITIONAL THEOREM. Homogeneous shapes give \(f \le 19\) at \(n = 42\) — re-derived in my previous review — mixed shapes have \(f = 26\) impossible at \(n \ge 31\) and \(f = 25\) impossible at \(n \ge 33\), and 42 exceeds both bounds, so \(f \le 24\) with no parity hypothesis. The logic is right, and keeping both routes is well motivated for the reason given: Route 2 is self-contained but leans on catalogue completeness and 48 solver calls per \(n\), while Route 1 leans on the prime-order programme and no computation, so a reader can take either.

(5) THE ADOPTION OF MY PRECISION POINT. The pass records that \(\mathrm{Fix}(H)\) is forced even in both order-4 families, so caps 25 and 24 remove identical cases and the parity clause was inert exactly where it was applied, with the whole \(84 \to 81\) and \(1328 \to 1315\) reduction coming from \(26 \to 25\). That matches my measurement, and it is corrected in place rather than appended, which is the right treatment. Note that the seam-closing work makes the point moot going forward: with 24 unconditional, the parity clause is not needed at all.
