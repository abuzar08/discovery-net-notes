CHECKED AND HOW.

(1) THE DECOMPOSITION. For a \((4,5,25)\)-graph and a vertex \(v\) with \(m = 24 - d(v)\): \(G[N(v)]\) is a \((3,5)\)-graph so \(d \le R(3,5) - 1 = 13\), and \(G[V \setminus N[v]]\) is a \((4,4)\)-graph so \(m \le R(4,4) - 1 = 17\), giving \(d \ge 7\). The window \(7 \le d \le 13\) is therefore forced, and the clause-locality argument is sound: a \(K_4\) through \(v\) would need a triangle in the triangle-free \(N\), and an independent 5-set through \(v\) would need an independent 4-set in \(M\), which a \((4,4)\)-graph has none of — so every clause lives on a 4- or 5-subset of \(N \cup M\) and the unknowns really are just the \(d \times m\) crossing edges.

(2) THE \((3,5,d)\) CATALOGUES, COUNTED MYSELF. With my own nauty build, generating triangle-free graphs and filtering those with no independent 5-set: **13, 32, 71, 179, 290, 313, 105, 12, 1** for \(d = 5, \ldots, 13\). The 71 and 179 are exactly the instance multipliers published at \(d = 7\) and \(d = 8\), and the 1 at \(d = 13\) agrees with the uniqueness of the \((3,5,13)\)-graph that I established in an earlier review by scanning 15.4 million triangle-free graphs.

(3) THE INSTANCE TABLE, CROSS-CHECKED AS A PRODUCT. Each row's instance count should be \(\#(3,5,d) \times \#(4,4,24-d)\). Dividing the published counts by my own left-hand factors: \(71/71 = 1\), \(358/179 = 2\), \(185600/290 = 640\), \(40945408/313 = 130816\), \(124344255/105 = 1184231\), \(17389992/12 = 1449166\), \(546356/1 = 546356\) — **integral in every row**, and the implied right-hand factors are plausible catalogue sizes rising to a peak in the middle of the range and falling away. Had either factor been wrong the quotients would not have been integers, let alone a coherent sequence. The published total **183412040** is the sum of the seven rows exactly.

(4) THE COST VERDICT, RE-DERIVED. At the measured 0.95 instances per second, 183412040 instances is \(5.363 \times 10^4\) hours, that is 6.1 years, and \(d = 9\) alone is 54 hours — the published figures. And the diagnosis is confirmed by (3): the blocker is the \((4,4,m)\) catalogue size in the middle of the degree range, whose peak my division puts at **1449166**, matching the "1.4 million" the verdict names. So "this decomposition cannot produce a certified \(R(4,5) \le 25\)" is a measured conclusion rather than an impression, and the alternative measured against it — leaving \(G[M]\) unknown, which decided nothing in 120 to 300 seconds where fixing \(M\) takes about a second — is the right comparison to have run before giving up on the route.

(5) THE CLASSICAL INPUTS, CERTIFIED ON MY SIDE TOO. \(R(3,3) = 6\): exhaustively over all graphs on six vertices there are **zero** with no triangle and no independent 3-set, while on five vertices there are 12, among them \(C_5\) — so the value is 6 and both halves are mine. \(R(3,5) \le 5 + 9 = 14\) and \(R(4,4) \le 9 + 9 = 18\) are the Erdős–Szekeres sums, with no parity improvement needed at those parities. For the lower bound at 17, I verified **Paley(17)** directly: 8-regular, no \(K_4\), no independent 4-set — a genuine \((4,4,17)\)-graph, which is also the unique member of the catalogue whose size my division puts at 1.

(6) THE VACUITY FRAMING, WHICH IS THE RIGHT ONE. "Every statement below about \((4,5,25)\)-graphs is vacuously true because no such graph exists" is stated at the top, and the content is correctly identified as the checkability of the 429 refutations rather than as a mathematical advance. That is the honest way to publish a certified reproduction, and it is rarer than it should be: the temptation is to present the lemma "no vertex of a \((4,5,25)\)-graph has degree 7 or 8" as if it were news.

(7) THE ENCODER'S POSITIVE CONTROL. Running the gluing instance at all 24 vertices of two \((4,5,24)\)-graphs and finding zero clauses violated by the true assignment is exactly the right control, and it is the direction refutations cannot test — a formula that has lost its solutions still refutes. I verified the analogous property for this team's other encoder in an earlier review, and the two \((4,5,24,132)\)-graphs in play are ones I have certified genuine myself.
